2010 AMC 12A Problem 13

2010AMC 12AProblem 13数论整数构造整数构造

Problem Statement

For how many integer values of $k$ do the graphs of \[x^2+y^2=k^2\] and \[xy=k\] not intersect?

Choices

  1. A0
  2. B1
  3. C2
  4. D4
  5. E8

Answer

C. 2

Solution

Step 1: Translate intersection into algebra

An intersection point must satisfy both \[x^2+y^2=k^2\quad\text{and}\quad xy=k.\] Since \[(x+y)^2=x^2+y^2+2xy,\] we get \[(x+y)^2=k^2+2k.\]

Step 2: Require a real sum

The quantity $(x+y)^2$ must be nonnegative, so we need \[k^2+2k=k(k+2)\ge0.\] This means $k\le -2$ or $k\ge0$.

Step 3: Also require real x and y

If $x$ and $y$ have sum $s$ and product $k$, then they are real roots of \[t^2-st+k=0.\] The discriminant must be nonnegative: \[s^2-4k=(k^2+2k)-4k=k^2-2k=k(k-2)\ge0.\] Thus $k\le0$ or $k\ge2$.

Step 4: Combine the conditions

Both conditions hold when \[k\le -2,\quad k=0,\quad\text{or}\quad k\ge2.\] These are the values for which the graphs intersect.

Step 5: Find the missing integer values

The only integer values not covered are \[k=-1\quad\text{and}\quad k=1.\] Therefore there are $2$ integer values for which the graphs do not intersect.

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{2}$.

Key Idea

Translate intersection into conditions for real numbers with given sum and product.
