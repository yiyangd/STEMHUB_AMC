2006 AMC 12B Problem 19

2006AMC 12BProblem 19数论进制与数字数字进制

Problem Statement

Mr. Jones has eight children of different ages. On a family trip his oldest child, who is \(9\), spots a license plate with a \(4\)-digit number in which each of two digits appears two times. \"Look, daddy!\" she exclaims. \"That number is evenly divisible by the age of each of us kids!\" \"That's right,\" replies Mr. Jones, \"and the last two digits just happen to be my age.\" Which of the following is not the age of one of Mr. Jones's children?

Choices

  1. A4
  2. B5
  3. C6
  4. D7
  5. E8

Answer

B. 5

Solution

Step 1: Use the age information

The eight children have distinct ages, and the oldest is \(9\). Therefore their ages are eight of the numbers \(1,2,\ldots,9\). We need determine which one is missing.

Step 2: Use divisibility by all child ages

The license plate number must be divisible by the ages that are present. For each possible missing age, it must be divisible by the least common multiple of the other eight ages.

Step 3: Use the repeated-digit condition

The plate is a four-digit number made from two digits, each repeated twice. Checking the possible least common multiples against this digit pattern leaves only one possible plate: \(5544\).

Step 4: Check the divisibility

The number \(5544\) is divisible by \(1,2,3,4,6,7,8,\) and \(9\), but not by \(5\).

Step 5: Interpret the result

Thus the eight children's ages are \(1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9\), so \(5\) is not one of the ages.

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is \(\boxed{5}\).

Key Idea

Use divisibility by the children's ages to identify the unique license plate.
