2011 AMC 12A Problem 10

2011AMC 12AProblem 10概率与统计几何概率几何概率面积

Problem Statement

A pair of standard $6$-sided fair dice is rolled once. The sum of the numbers rolled determines the diameter of a circle. What is the probability that the numerical value of the area of the circle is less than the numerical value of the circle's circumference?

Choices

  1. A$\frac1{36}$
  2. B$\frac1{12}$
  3. C$\frac16$
  4. D$\frac14$
  5. E$\frac5{18}$

Answer

B. \frac1{12}

Solution

Step 1: Write area and circumference using diameter

If the diameter is $d$, then the radius is $\frac d2$. The area is \[\pi\left(\frac d2\right)^2=\frac{\pi d^2}{4},\] and the circumference is \[\pi d.\]

Step 2: Compare the two quantities

We need \[\frac{\pi d^2}{4}<\pi d.\] Since $d>0$, divide by $\pi d$ to get \[\frac d4<1,\] so $d<4$.

Step 3: Translate to dice sums

The diameter is the sum of the dice, so we need a sum less than $4$. The possible sums are $2$ and $3$.

Step 4: Count outcomes

There is $1$ way to roll a sum of $2$ and $2$ ways to roll a sum of $3$, for $3$ favorable outcomes out of $36$ total.

Step 5: Compute the probability

The probability is \[\frac3{36}=\frac1{12}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is \[\boxed{\frac1{12}}.\]

Key Idea

Compare circle area and circumference in terms of the diameter.
