2016 AMC 12A Problem 18

2016AMC 12AProblem 18数论整数构造整数构造

Problem Statement

For some positive integer $n$, the number $110n^3$ has $110$ positive integer divisors, including $1$ and the number $110n^3$. How many positive integer divisors does the number $81n^4$ have?

Choices

  1. A110
  2. B191
  3. C261
  4. D325
  5. E425

Answer

D. 325

Solution

Step 1: Translate divisor count into exponents

If a number has prime factorization \(\prod p_i^{e_i}\), then its number of divisors is \(\prod(e_i+1)\). Here \[110=2\cdot5\cdot11.\]

Step 2: Look at primes already in 110

For the primes $2$, $5$, and $11$, if the exponent of that prime in $n$ is $k$, then its exponent in $110n^3$ is $1+3k$. The corresponding divisor-count factor is \[1+3k+1=3k+2.\]

Step 3: Use the allowed factors of 110

The only factors available in $110=2\cdot5\cdot11$ that are congruent to $2$ modulo $3$ are $2$, $5$, and $11$. Therefore the exponents $k$ in $n$ for the primes $2,5,11$ must be $0$, $1$, and $3$ in some order.

Step 4: Exclude extra primes

Any other prime in $n$ with exponent $k>0$ would contribute a factor $3k+1$ to the divisor count of $110n^3$. No such factor can fit into $110$, so there are no other primes in $n$.

Step 5: Count divisors of the new number

In $81n^4=3^4n^4$, the new prime $3$ contributes a divisor-count factor of $4+1=5$. The exponents from $n^4$ are $4\cdot0$, $4\cdot1$, and $4\cdot3$, contributing factors \[1,\quad5,\quad13.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The number of divisors is \[5\cdot1\cdot5\cdot13=\boxed{325}.\]

Key Idea

Use divisor-count factors to determine the possible prime exponents in n.
