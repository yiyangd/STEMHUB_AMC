Problem Statement

The measures of the smallest angles of three different right triangles sum to $90^\circ$. All three triangles have side lengths that are primitive Pythagorean triples. Two of them are $3$-$4$-$5$ and $5$-$12$-$13$. What is the perimeter of the third triangle?

Choices

  1. A40
  2. B126
  3. C154
  4. D176
  5. E208

Answer

C. 154

Solution

Step 1: Convert the known angles to tangents

For the $3$-$4$-$5$ triangle, the smallest angle has tangent $\frac34$. For the $5$-$12$-$13$ triangle, the smallest angle has tangent $\frac5{12}$.

Let these two angles be $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

Step 2: Find the tangent of their sum

Using the tangent addition formula,

\[\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\frac{\frac34+ rac5{12}}{1- rac34\cdot rac5{12}}.\]

The numerator is $\frac{14}{12}$, and the denominator is $1- rac{15}{48}=\frac{33}{48}$. So

\[\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\frac{56}{33}.\]

Step 3: Use the fact that the three angles sum to 90 degrees

If the third smallest angle is $\gamma$, then

\[\gamma=90^\circ-(\alpha+eta).\]

Therefore

\[\tan\gamma=\cot(lpha+eta)=\frac{33}{56}.\]

Step 4: Identify the primitive Pythagorean triangle

A right triangle whose smallest angle has tangent $\frac{33}{56}$ has legs $33$ and $56$. The hypotenuse is

\[\sqrt{33^2+56^2}=65.\]

This is a primitive Pythagorean triple.

Step 5: Compute the perimeter

The perimeter is

\[33+56+65=154.\]

So the answer is $154$.

Key Idea

Use tangent addition for the two known small angles, then identify the third primitive Pythagorean triangle.
