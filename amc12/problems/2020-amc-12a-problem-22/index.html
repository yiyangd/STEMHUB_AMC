2020 AMC 12A Problem 22

2020AMC 12AProblem 22组合数学递推与序列计数递推序列

Problem Statement

Let $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ be the sequences of real numbers such that \[(2+i)^n=a_n+b_ni\] for all integers $n\ge0$, where $i=\sqrt{-1}$. What is \[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_nb_n}{7^n}?\]

Choices

  1. A$\frac38$
  2. B$\frac7{16}$
  3. C$\frac12$
  4. D$\frac9{16}$
  5. E$\frac47$

Answer

B. \frac7{16}

Solution

Step 1: Relate a_n b_n to a complex square

If \[(2+i)^n=a_n+b_ni,\] then \[(a_n+b_ni)^2=a_n^2-b_n^2+2a_nb_ni.\] So \[a_nb_n=\frac12\operatorname{Im}\left((2+i)^{2n}\right).\]

Step 2: Simplify the base

We have \[(2+i)^2=3+4i.\] Therefore \[\frac{a_nb_n}{7^n}=\frac12\operatorname{Im}\left(\left(\frac{3+4i}{7}\right)^n\right).\]

Step 3: Use a geometric series

Because \[\left|\frac{3+4i}{7}\right|=\frac57<1,\] the infinite series converges, and \[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{3+4i}{7}\right)^n=\frac1{1-\frac{3+4i}{7}}=\frac{7}{4-4i}.\]

Step 4: Find the imaginary part

Rationalize: \[\frac{7}{4-4i}=\frac{7(4+4i)}{32}=\frac78+\frac78i.\] Its imaginary part is $\frac78$.

Step 5: Conclude

The desired sum is half of that imaginary part: \[\frac12\cdot\frac78=\frac7{16}.\] The answer is $\boxed{\frac7{16}}$.

Key Idea

Convert a product of real and imaginary parts into the imaginary part of a geometric series.
