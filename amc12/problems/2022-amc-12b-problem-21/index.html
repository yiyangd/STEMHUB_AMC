2022 AMC 12B Problem 21

2022AMC 12BProblem 21几何坐标几何坐标几何

Problem Statement

Let $S$ be the set of circles in the coordinate plane that are tangent to each of the three circles with equations \[x^2+y^2=4,\quad x^2+y^2=64,\quad (x-5)^2+y^2=3.\] What is the sum of the areas of all circles in $S$?

Choices

  1. A$48\pi$
  2. B$68\pi$
  3. C$96\pi$
  4. D$102\pi$
  5. E$136\pi$

Answer

E. $136\pi$

Solution

Step 1: First use the two concentric circles

A circle tangent to both $x^2+y^2=4$ and $x^2+y^2=64$ must be tangent to circles centered at the origin with radii $2$ and $8$.

Step 2: Find the possible radii

There are two possible types. A circle inside the annulus has center distance $5$ from the origin and radius $3$, because $5-3=2$ and $5+3=8$. A circle containing the smaller circle internally has center distance $3$ and radius $5$, because $5-3=2$ and $5+3=8$.

Step 3: Use the third circle

The third circle has center $(5,0)$ and radius $\sqrt3$. For each of the two possible radii, the new center must also lie on a circle centered at $(5,0)$ with radius $r+\sqrt3$ or $|r-\sqrt3|$.

Step 4: Count the possible circles

For radius $3$, the center is on the circle of radius $5$ about the origin, and the two tangency choices with the third circle each give two intersections, for $4$ circles. The same reasoning gives $4$ circles of radius $5$.

Step 5: Add the areas

The total area is \[4\pi(3^2)+4\pi(5^2)=36\pi+100\pi=136\pi.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{136\pi}$.

Key Idea

Use tangency to the two concentric circles to determine possible radii.
