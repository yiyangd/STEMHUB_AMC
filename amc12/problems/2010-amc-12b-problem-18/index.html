2010 AMC 12B Problem 18

2010AMC 12BProblem 18概率与统计组合概率概率计数

Problem Statement

A frog makes $3$ jumps, each exactly $1$ meter long. The directions of the jumps are chosen independently and at random. What is the probability the frog's final position is no more than $1$ meter from its starting position?

Choices

  1. A$\frac16$
  2. B$\frac15$
  3. C$\frac14$
  4. D$\frac13$
  5. E$\frac12$

Answer

C. \frac14

Solution

Step 1: Think of directions as points on a circle

Each jump is a unit vector in a random direction. We need the length of the sum of the three unit vectors to be at most $1$.

Step 2: Use a geometric criterion

For three unit vectors, the sum has length at most $1$ exactly when the three directions are not all contained in one open semicircle. If they are all in one semicircle, their pull is too concentrated in one direction; if not, they wrap around the origin enough to keep the resultant within distance $1$.

Step 3: Count the complement

For $3$ random points on a circle, the probability that all lie in some semicircle is \[\frac{3}{2^{3-1}}=\frac34.\] One way to see this is to choose which of the three points is the first endpoint of a semicircle containing the other two.

Step 4: Take the complement

Therefore the probability that the directions are not all in a semicircle is \[1-\frac34=\frac14.\]

Step 5: Conclude

The answer is \[\boxed{\frac14}.\]

Key Idea

Reinterpret three random directions on a circle and use the semicircle criterion.
