2009 AMC 12B Problem 18

2009AMC 12BProblem 18概率与统计组合概率概率计数

Problem Statement

Rachel and Robert run on a circular track. Rachel runs counterclockwise and completes one lap every $90$ seconds, while Robert runs clockwise and completes one lap every $80$ seconds. Both start at the same point. At a random time between $10$ and $11$ minutes after they start, a photographer takes a picture of the quarter of the track centered at the starting point. What is the probability that both runners are in the picture?

Choices

  1. A$\frac18$
  2. B$\frac5{32}$
  3. C$\frac3{16}$
  4. D$\frac7{32}$
  5. E$\frac14$

Answer

C. \frac3{16}

Solution

Step 1: Convert the picture condition into positions on a circle

The photographed quarter of the track is centered at the starting point. That means a runner is visible exactly when the runner is within $\frac18$ of a lap on either side of the start point.

Step 2: Set up the time window

We only look between $10$ and $11$ minutes, or from $600$ to $660$ seconds. Rachel has period $90$ seconds and Robert has period $80$ seconds, so their positions repeat modulo $90$ and modulo $80$ respectively.

Step 3: Find Rachel's visible times

In this minute, Rachel is near the start when her time modulo $90$ is between $0$ and $11.25$ seconds, or between $78.75$ and $90$ seconds. This gives visible intervals that include $[630,641.25]$ in the time window.

Step 4: Find Robert's visible times

Robert is near the start when his time modulo $80$ is between $0$ and $10$ seconds, or between $70$ and $80$ seconds. In the same time window, the relevant visible intervals include $[630,640]$ and $[640,650]$.

Step 5: Intersect the intervals

Both runners are visible during \[[630,640]\quad\text{and}\quad[640,641.25].\] The total favorable time is \[10+1.25=\frac{45}{4}\] seconds.

Step 6: Convert to probability

The full window has length $60$ seconds, so the probability is \[\frac{45/4}{60}=\frac3{16}.\] The answer is \[\boxed{\frac3{16}}.\]

Key Idea

Convert runner positions into time intervals inside the photographed arc.
