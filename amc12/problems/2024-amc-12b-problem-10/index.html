2024 AMC 12B Problem 10

2024AMC 12BProblem 10概率与统计平均数与数据集平均数中位数极差有序三元组

Problem Statement

A list of $9$ real numbers consists of $1,2.2,3.2,5.2,6.2,7$, as well as $x,y,z$ with $x\le y\le z$. The range of the list is $7$, and the mean and median are both positive integers. How many ordered triples $(x,y,z)$ are possible?

Choices

  1. A1
  2. B2
  3. C3
  4. D4
  5. Einfinitely many

Answer

C. 3

Solution

Step 1: Start with the fixed numbers

The six fixed numbers have sum

\[1+2.2+3.2+5.2+6.2+7=24.8.\]

The range of these fixed numbers alone is $6$, so the new numbers must extend the range to exactly $7$.

Step 2: List the range possibilities

Because the fixed minimum and maximum are $1$ and $7$, the final range can be $7$ in three ways: the minimum is $0$ and the maximum remains $7$; the maximum is $8$ and the minimum remains $1$; or one new value is below $1$ and another is exactly $7$ more than it.

Step 3: Use the mean condition

The total sum must be a positive integer multiple of $9$. This strongly restricts $x+y+z$, because the fixed sum is $24.8$.

We can now test the three range cases while also requiring the median, the fifth number in sorted order, to be a positive integer.

Step 4: Count the endpoint cases

If $x=0$ and $z\le7$, the only possibility is

\[(x,y,z)=(0,5,6.2).\]

If $z=8$ and $x\ge1$, the only possibility is

\[(x,y,z)=(6,6.2,8).\]

Both have integer mean and integer median.

Step 5: Count the split-beyond-both-ends case

If $x<1$ and $z=x+7>7$, then the median can be an integer only when $y=4$ or $y=5$. The mean condition leaves the valid triple

\[(x,y,z)=(0.1,4,7.1).\]

The other candidate would not keep the true range equal to $7$.

Step 6: Finish the count

There are exactly three ordered triples:

\[(0,5,6.2),\quad (6,6.2,8),\quad (0.1,4,7.1).\]

Therefore the answer is $3$.

Key Idea

Use the range condition to split into endpoint cases, then impose integer mean and median.
