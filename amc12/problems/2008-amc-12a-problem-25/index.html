2008 AMC 12A Problem 25

2008AMC 12AProblem 25几何坐标几何坐标几何

Problem Statement

A sequence \((a_1,b_1),(a_2,b_2),(a_3,b_3),\ldots\) of points in the coordinate plane satisfies \[(a_{n+1},b_{n+1})=(\sqrt3a_n-b_n,\sqrt3b_n+a_n)\] for \(n=1,2,3,\ldots\). Suppose that \((a_{100},b_{100})=(2,4)\). What is \(a_1+b_1\)?

Choices

  1. A\(\frac1{2^{97}}\)
  2. B\(\frac1{2^{99}}\)
  3. C0
  4. D\(\frac1{2^{98}}\)
  5. E\(\frac1{2^{96}}\)

Answer

D. \frac{1}{2^{98}}

Solution

Step 1: Use complex numbers

Let \(z_n=a_n+b_ni\). The recurrence becomes \[z_{n+1}=(a_n+b_ni)(\sqrt3+i)=z_n(\sqrt3+i).\]

Step 2: Write the multiplier in polar form

\(\sqrt3+i=2\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{6}+i\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\). Each step multiplies the modulus by \(2\) and rotates by \(30^\circ\).

Step 3: Relate z100 to z1

Since going from \(z_1\) to \(z_{100}\) takes \(99\) multiplications, \[z_{100}=z_1(\sqrt3+i)^{99}.\]

Step 4: Simplify the 99th power

\[(\sqrt3+i)^{99}=2^{99}\left(\cos\frac{99\pi}{6}+i\sin\frac{99\pi}{6}\right)=2^{99}i,\] because \(\frac{99\pi}{6}=\frac{33\pi}{2}\).

Step 5: Solve for z1

Given \(z_{100}=2+4i\), \[z_1=\frac{2+4i}{2^{99}i}=\frac{4-2i}{2^{99}}.\] Thus \(a_1=\frac4{2^{99}}\) and \(b_1=-\frac2{2^{99}}\).

Step 6: Add the coordinates

\[a_1+b_1=\frac{4-2}{2^{99}}=\frac2{2^{99}}=\frac1{2^{98}}.\]

Step 7: Conclude

The answer is \(\boxed{\frac1{2^{98}}}\).

Key Idea

Represent the recurrence as multiplication by \(\sqrt3+i\) in the complex plane.
