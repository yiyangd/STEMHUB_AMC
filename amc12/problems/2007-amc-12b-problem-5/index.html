2007 AMC 12B Problem 5

Problem Statement

The 2007 AMC 12 contests will be scored by awarding \(6\) points for each correct response, \(0\) points for each incorrect response, and \(1.5\) points for each problem left unanswered. After looking over the \(25\) problems, Sarah has decided to attempt the first \(22\) and leave the last \(3\) unanswered. How many of the first \(22\) problems must she solve correctly in order to score at least \(100\) points?

Choices

  1. A13
  2. B14
  3. C15
  4. D16
  5. E17

Answer

D. 16

Solution

Step 1: Account for the unanswered problems first

Sarah leaves \(3\) problems unanswered. Each unanswered problem gives \(1.5\) points, so these contribute \[3\cdot1.5=4.5\] points.

Step 2: Find the score needed from correct answers

To reach at least \(100\), she needs at least \[100-4.5=95.5\] points from the first \(22\) attempted problems.

Step 3: Translate correct answers into points

Each correct answer is worth \(6\) points, while incorrect answers give \(0\). If she solves \(c\) of the first \(22\) correctly, then she earns \(6c\) points from them.

Step 4: Solve the inequality

We need \[6c\ge95.5.\] Since \(\frac{95.5}{6}\approx15.92\), the least integer \(c\) is \(16\).

Step 5: Conclude

Sarah must solve \(\boxed{16}\) of the first \(22\) problems correctly.

Key Idea

Account for unanswered-point credit before solving the score inequality.
