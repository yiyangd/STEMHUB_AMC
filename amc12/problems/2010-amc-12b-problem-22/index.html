2010 AMC 12B Problem 22

2010AMC 12BProblem 22几何三角形三角形角度

Problem Statement

Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral. The side lengths of $ABCD$ are distinct integers less than $15$ such that $BC\cdot CD=AB\cdot DA$. What is the largest possible value of $BD$?

Choices

  1. A$\sqrt{\frac{325}{2}}$
  2. B$\sqrt{185}$
  3. C$\sqrt{\frac{389}{2}}$
  4. D$\sqrt{\frac{425}{2}}$
  5. E$\sqrt{\frac{533}{2}}$

Answer

D. $\sqrt{\frac{425}{2}}$

Solution

Step 1: Name the sides and use the cyclic condition

Let

\[AB=a,\quad BC=b,\quad CD=c,\quad DA=d.\]

The condition in the problem is

\[bc=ad.\]

Because $ABCD$ is cyclic, opposite angles are supplementary, so

\[\cos\angle BAD=-\cos\angle BCD.\]

Step 2: Apply the Law of Cosines in two triangles

Use diagonal $\overline{BD}$ to split the quadrilateral into $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle BCD$.

In $\triangle ABD$,

\[BD^2=a^2+d^2-2ad\cos\angle BAD.\]

In $\triangle BCD$,

\[BD^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos\angle BCD.\]

Step 3: Add the two equations

Since $ad=bc$ and the cosines have opposite signs, the cosine terms cancel when the two equations are added.

Thus

\[2BD^2=a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2.\]

So maximizing $BD$ is the same as maximizing the sum of the squares of the four side lengths, subject to $ad=bc$ and the sides being distinct integers less than $15$.

Step 4: Do an organized factor check

The largest side should be included if possible, so test side length $14$. With $a=14$, the equation becomes

\[14d=bc.\]

The largest valid distinct factor choice under $15$ is

\[d=6,\qquad b=7,\qquad c=12,\]

because $14\cdot6=7\cdot12=84$. A finite check of the other factor pairs with side lengths below $15$ gives a smaller square-sum.

Step 5: Compute the diagonal

For the side lengths $14,7,12,6$ in cyclic order satisfying the product condition,

\[2BD^2=14^2+7^2+12^2+6^2.\]

Therefore

\[2BD^2=196+49+144+36=425.\]

So

\[BD^2=\frac{425}{2}.\]

Step 6: Finish

The largest possible value of the diagonal is

\[BD=\sqrt{\frac{425}{2}}.\]

Therefore the answer is $\sqrt{\frac{425}{2}}$.

Key Idea

Use supplementary angles in a cyclic quadrilateral to express the diagonal in terms of the four sides.
