Problem Statement

How many values of $\theta$ in the interval $0<\theta\le2\pi$ satisfy \[1-3\sin\theta+5\cos3\theta=0?\]

Choices

  1. A2
  2. B4
  3. C5
  4. D6
  5. E8

Answer

D. 6

Solution

Step 1: Use a substitution that avoids graph guessing

The expression contains both $\sin\theta$ and $\cos3\theta$, so direct factoring is not obvious. A reliable way to count roots is the tangent half-angle substitution

\[t=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}.\]

This gives a one-to-one correspondence from $0<\theta<\pi$ to $t>0$ and from $\pi<\theta<2\pi$ to $t<0$.

Step 2: Convert the equation to a polynomial

Using

\[\sin\theta=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\qquad \cos heta=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\]

and $\cos3\theta=4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta$, the equation becomes

\[2t^6+3t^5-39t^4+6t^3+36t^2+3t-3=0.\]

So the original problem has no more than $6$ solutions away from the endpoints.

Step 3: Show that six real roots actually occur

Let

\[P(t)=2t^6+3t^5-39t^4+6t^3+36t^2+3t-3.\]

A sign check gives changes of sign on the six disjoint intervals

\[(-6,-5),\quad (-1,-\tfrac12),\quad (-\tfrac25,-\tfrac14),\] \[(0,\tfrac12),\quad (1,2),\quad (3,4).\]

By the Intermediate Value Theorem, $P(t)$ has at least one real root in each interval.

Step 4: Use the degree to finish the count

Because $P(t)$ is a degree-$6$ polynomial, it can have at most $6$ real roots. The six sign-change intervals already force at least $6$ real roots, so it has exactly $6$ real roots.

Step 5: Check the endpoints and translate back

The endpoints do not add extra solutions: at $\theta=2\pi$, the left side is $1+5=6$, and $\theta=0$ is not included. Also $\theta=\pi$ gives $1+5\cos3\pi=-4$, so it is not a solution.

Thus the number of values of $\theta$ is $6$.

Key Idea

Use the tangent half-angle substitution to turn the trigonometric equation into a polynomial root count.
