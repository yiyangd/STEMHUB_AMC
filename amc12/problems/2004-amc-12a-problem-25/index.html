2004 AMC 12A Problem 25

2004AMC 12AProblem 25数论进制与数字数字进制

Problem Statement

For each integer \(n\ge4\), let \(a_n\) denote the base-\(n\) number \(0.\overline{133}_n\). The product \(a_4a_5\cdots a_{99}\) can be expressed as \(\frac{m}{N!}\), where \(m\) and \(N\) are positive integers and \(N\) is as small as possible. What is the value of \(m\)?

Choices

  1. A98
  2. B101
  3. C132
  4. D798
  5. E962

Answer

E. 962

Solution

Step 1: Convert the repeating decimal

In base \(n\), \(0.\overline{133}_n\) has repeating block \(133\). Therefore \[a_n=\frac{n^2+3n+3}{n^3-1}.\]

Step 2: Prepare for telescoping

Notice that \(n^2+3n+3=(n+1)^2+(n+1)+1\). Also \[n^3-1=(n-1)(n^2+n+1).\]

Step 3: Rewrite each factor

Let \(Q_n=n^2+n+1\). Then \[a_n=\frac{Q_{n+1}}{(n-1)Q_n}.\]

Step 4: Multiply from 4 to 99

The \(Q_n\) factors telescope: \[\prod_{n=4}^{99}a_n=\frac{Q_{100}}{Q_4\cdot(3\cdot4\cdots98)}.\]

Step 5: Simplify the product

We have \(Q_{100}=100^2+100+1=10101\) and \(Q_4=21\). Also \(3\cdot4\cdots98=\frac{98!}{2}\). Thus \[\prod_{n=4}^{99}a_n=\frac{10101}{21}\cdot\frac{2}{98!}=\frac{962}{98!}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

With \(N\) as small as possible, \(m=\boxed{962}\).

Key Idea

Convert the repeating base-\(n\) decimal into a telescoping product.
