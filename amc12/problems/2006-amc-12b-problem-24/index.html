2006 AMC 12B Problem 24

2006AMC 12BProblem 24几何坐标几何坐标几何

Problem Statement

Let \(S\) be the set of all points \((x,y)\) in the coordinate plane such that \(0\le x\le\frac{\pi}{2}\) and \(0\le y\le\frac{\pi}{2}\). What is the area of the subset of \(S\) for which \[\sin^2x-\sin x\sin y+\sin^2y\le\frac34?\]

Choices

  1. A\(\frac{\pi^2}{9}\)
  2. B\(\frac{\pi^2}{8}\)
  3. C\(\frac{\pi^2}{6}\)
  4. D\(\frac{3\pi^2}{16}\)
  5. E\(\frac{2\pi^2}{9}\)

Answer

C. \frac{\pi^2}{6}

Solution

Step 1: Solve the boundary first

The boundary is \[\sin^2x-\sin x\sin y+\sin^2y=\frac34.\] Treating this as a quadratic in \(\sin x\) gives \[\sin x=\frac12\sin y\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cos y.\]

Step 2: Recognize angle formulas

The right side is exactly \(\sin(y\pm\frac{\pi}{3})\). So the boundary in the square is made from the lines \[x=y+\frac{\pi}{3},\quad x=y-\frac{\pi}{3},\quad x=\frac{2\pi}{3}-y,\] restricted to \(0\le x,y\le\frac{\pi}{2}\).

Step 3: Choose the correct side

Testing a point such as \((\frac{\pi}{6},\frac{\pi}{6})\) shows it satisfies the inequality. Thus the desired region is the main pentagonal region left after cutting off three corner triangles from the square.

Step 4: Compute from the square

The full square has area \[\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^2=\frac{\pi^2}{4}.\] The removed pieces are two right triangles with legs \(\frac{\pi}{6}\), and one right triangle with legs \(\frac{\pi}{3}\).

Step 5: Subtract

The desired area is \[\frac{\pi^2}{4}-2\cdot\frac12\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^2-\frac12\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)^2=\pi^2\left(\frac14-\frac1{36}-\frac1{18}\right)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The area is \(\boxed{\frac{\pi^2}{6}}\).

Key Idea

Rewrite the boundary with sine angle identities, then compute a polygonal area.
