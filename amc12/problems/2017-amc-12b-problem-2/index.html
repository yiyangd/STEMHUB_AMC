2017 AMC 12B Problem 2

2017AMC 12BProblem 2代数方程与不等式方程不等式

Problem Statement

Real numbers $x$, $y$, and $z$ satisfy \(0<x<1\), \(-1<y<0\), and \(1<z<2\). Which of the following numbers is necessarily positive?

Choices

  1. A$y+x^2$
  2. B$y+xz$
  3. C$y+y^2$
  4. D$y+2y^2$
  5. E$y+z$

Answer

E. y+z

Solution

Step 1: Use the ranges

We know \(y\) is negative but greater than \(-1\), while \(z\) is greater than \(1\).

Step 2: Test the guaranteed expression

For \(y+z\), the smallest possible behavior is just above \(-1+1=0\). Since both inequalities are strict, \[y+z>0.\]

Step 3: See why the others can fail

Expressions such as \(y+x^2\) or \(y+xz\) can be negative if \(y\) is close to \(-1\) and \(x\) is very small. Expressions involving \(y+y^2\) also need not be positive for \(-1<y<0\).

Step 4: Conclude

The necessarily positive expression is $\boxed{y+z}$.

Key Idea

Use interval bounds to identify an expression that must be positive.
