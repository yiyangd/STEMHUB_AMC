Problem Statement

Cyrus the frog jumps $2$ units in a random direction, then $2$ more units in another random direction. What is the probability that he lands less than $1$ unit away from his starting position?

Choices

  1. A$\frac16$
  2. B$\frac15$
  3. C$\frac{\sqrt3}{8}$
  4. D$\frac{\arctan(1/2)}{\pi}$
  5. E$\frac{2\arcsin(1/4)}{\pi}$

Answer

E. $\frac{2\arcsin(1/4)}{\pi}$

Solution

Step 1: Use the angle between jumps

After the first jump, only the angle $\theta$ between the first and second jump directions matters. This angle is uniformly distributed from $0$ to $2\pi$.

Step 2: Write the final distance

By the Law of Cosines, the squared distance from the start is \[2^2+2^2+2\cdot2\cdot2\cos\theta=8+8\cos\theta.\]

Step 3: Convert to a half-angle

Since \[8+8\cos\theta=16\cos^2\frac{\theta}{2},\] the final distance is \[4\left|\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right|.\]

Step 4: Apply the condition

We need \[4\left|\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right|<1,\] so \[\left|\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right|<\frac14.\]

Step 5: Measure the allowed angles

For $\phi=\frac{\theta}{2}$ in $[0,\pi)$, the allowed interval has length \[2\arcsin\frac14.\] Dividing by the total length $\pi$ gives the probability.

Step 6: Conclude

The probability is \[\boxed{\frac{2\arcsin(1/4)}{\pi}}.\]

Key Idea

Convert the two-jump distance to an angle condition.
