2007 AMC 12B Problem 23

2007AMC 12BProblem 23几何面积与相似面积比例

Problem Statement

How many non-congruent right triangles with positive integer leg lengths have areas that are numerically equal to \(3\) times their perimeters?

Choices

  1. A6
  2. B7
  3. C8
  4. D10
  5. E12

Answer

A. 6

Solution

Step 1: Connect area and perimeter to the inradius

For any triangle, area equals inradius times semiperimeter. If the area is \(3\) times the perimeter, then it is \(6\) times the semiperimeter. So the inradius is \(6\).

Step 2: Use the right-triangle inradius formula

For a right triangle with integer legs \(x,y\) and hypotenuse \(h\), the inradius is \[r=\frac{x+y-h}{2}.\] Since \(r=6\), we have \(x+y-h=12\), so \(h=x+y-12\).

Step 3: Use the Pythagorean theorem

Substitute \(h=x+y-12\) into \(x^2+y^2=h^2\): \[x^2+y^2=(x+y-12)^2.\]

Step 4: Simplify into a factor equation

Expanding and simplifying gives \[xy-12x-12y+72=0,\] or \[(x-12)(y-12)=72.\]

Step 5: Count unordered factor pairs

Positive integer factor pairs of \(72\), up to order, are \[(1,72),(2,36),(3,24),(4,18),(6,12),(8,9).\] Each gives a non-congruent right triangle.

Step 6: Conclude

There are \(\boxed{6}\) such triangles.

Key Idea

Use the inradius relation for right triangles with area equal to three times perimeter.
