Problem Statement

Driving at a constant speed, Sharon usually takes $180$ minutes to drive from her house to her mother's house. One day Sharon begins the drive at her usual speed, but after driving $\frac13$ of the way, she hits a snowstorm and reduces her speed by $20$ miles per hour. This time the trip takes her a total of $276$ minutes. How many miles is it from Sharon's house to her mother's house?

Choices

  1. A132
  2. B135
  3. C138
  4. D141
  5. E144

Answer

B. 135

Solution

Step 1: Use hours for time

The usual trip takes $180$ minutes, or $3$ hours. Let Sharon's usual speed be \(v\) miles per hour, so the total distance is \(3v\).

Step 2: Handle the first third

The first third of the trip is distance \(v\), so at her usual speed it takes exactly $1$ hour.

Step 3: Use the total storm-day time

The storm-day trip takes \(276\) minutes, or \(4.6\) hours. Therefore the last two thirds take \(4.6-1=3.6\) hours.

Step 4: Set up the slower-speed equation

The last two thirds have distance \(2v\), and the speed is \(v-20\). Thus \[\frac{2v}{v-20}=3.6.\]

Step 5: Solve and find the distance

This gives \[2v=3.6v-72,\] so \(1.6v=72\) and \(v=45\). The full distance is \[3v=135.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The distance is $\boxed{135}$ miles.

Key Idea

Translate the changed speed trip into time equations.
