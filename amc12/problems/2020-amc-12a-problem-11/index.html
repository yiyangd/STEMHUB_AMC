2020 AMC 12A Problem 11

2020AMC 12AProblem 11概率与统计几何概率几何概率面积

Problem Statement

A frog sitting at the point $(1,2)$ begins a sequence of jumps, where each jump is parallel to one of the coordinate axes and has length $1$, and the direction of each jump is chosen independently at random. The sequence ends when the frog reaches a side of the square with vertices $(0,0)$, $(0,4)$, $(4,4)$, and $(4,0)$. What is the probability that the sequence of jumps ends on a vertical side of the square?

Choices

  1. A$\frac12$
  2. B$\frac58$
  3. C$\frac23$
  4. D$\frac34$
  5. E$\frac78$

Answer

B. \frac58

Solution

Step 1: Set up hitting probabilities

Let $p(x,y)$ be the probability of eventually hitting a vertical side before a horizontal side, starting from $(x,y)$. Boundary values are $1$ on $x=0$ or $x=4$, and $0$ on $y=0$ or $y=4$.

Step 2: Use the averaging rule

At an interior lattice point, the next move is equally likely in four directions, so \[p(x,y)=\frac14\big(p(x+1,y)+p(x-1,y)+p(x,y+1)+p(x,y-1)\big).\]

Step 3: Exploit symmetry

The point $(1,2)$ lies on the horizontal middle line. Solving the small $3$-by-$3$ system, or using symmetry to reduce it, gives \[p(1,2)=\frac58.\]

Step 4: Interpret the result

This value is greater than $\frac12$ because the frog starts closer to the left vertical side than to either horizontal side.

Step 5: Conclude

The probability is $\boxed{\frac58}$.

Key Idea

Solve a small absorbing random walk using harmonic probabilities.
