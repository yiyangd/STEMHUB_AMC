2007 AMC 12B Problem 18

2007AMC 12BProblem 18几何三角形三角形角度

Problem Statement

Let \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) be digits with \(a\ne0\). The three-digit integer \(\overline{abc}\) lies one third of the way from the square of a positive integer to the square of the next larger integer. The integer \(\overline{acb}\) lies two thirds of the way between the same two squares. What is \(a+b+c\)?

Choices

  1. A10
  2. B13
  3. C16
  4. D18
  5. E21

Answer

C. 16

Solution

Step 1: Name the consecutive squares

Let the two consecutive squares be \(k^2\) and \((k+1)^2\). Their difference is \[(k+1)^2-k^2=2k+1.\]

Step 2: Translate the one-third and two-thirds positions

The number \(\overline{abc}\) is \[k^2+\frac{2k+1}{3},\] and \(\overline{acb}\) is \[k^2+\frac{2(2k+1)}{3}.\]

Step 3: Use the digit swap

The difference between \(\overline{acb}\) and \(\overline{abc}\) is \[(100a+10c+b)-(100a+10b+c)=9(c-b).\] This also equals \(\frac{2k+1}{3}\).

Step 4: Relate k to the digit difference

Thus \[9(c-b)=\frac{2k+1}{3},\] so \[27(c-b)=2k+1.\] Since the numbers are three-digit, testing possible digit differences quickly narrows the case.

Step 5: Find the only three-digit case

If \(c-b=1\), then \(2k+1=27\), so \(k=13\). Then \[\overline{abc}=13^2+9=178,\] which gives \(a=1\), \(b=7\), and \(c=8\). Larger positive digit differences make \(k^2\) too large for a three-digit number.

Step 6: Compute the requested sum

Therefore \[a+b+c=1+7+8=16.\]

Step 7: Conclude

The answer is \(\boxed{16}\).

Key Idea

Use the one-third and two-thirds positions between consecutive squares.
