2014 AMC 12B Problem 18

2014AMC 12BProblem 18组合数学排列组合排列组合计数

Problem Statement

The numbers $1,2,3,4,5$ are to be arranged in a circle. An arrangement is bad if it is not true that for every $n$ from $1$ to $15$ one can find a subset of the numbers that appear consecutively on the circle that sum to $n$. Arrangements that differ only by a rotation or a reflection are considered the same. How many different bad arrangements are there?

Choices

  1. A1
  2. B2
  3. C3
  4. D4
  5. E5

Answer

B. 2

Solution

Step 1: Reduce by symmetry

Up to rotation and reflection, there are only \[\frac{5!}{2\cdot5}=12\] circular arrangements to check.

Step 2: Use consecutive sums

For any arrangement, the possible sums come from arcs of lengths $1,2,3,4,$ and $5$ around the circle. The total sum is $15$, so missing a sum $t$ is equivalent to missing the complementary sum $15-t$.

Step 3: Check the twelve cases systematically

A short enumeration of the $12$ symmetry classes shows that exactly two classes fail the condition. Representatives are \[(1,2,5,3,4)\quad\text{and}\quad(1,3,2,4,5).\]

Step 4: Identify what is missing

For $(1,2,5,3,4)$, the missing sums are $6$ and $9$. For $(1,3,2,4,5)$, the missing sums are $7$ and $8$.

Step 5: Conclude

There are exactly $\boxed{2}$ bad arrangements.

Key Idea

Enumerate circular arrangements up to symmetry and check consecutive sums.
