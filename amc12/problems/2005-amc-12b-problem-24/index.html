2005 AMC 12B Problem 24

2005AMC 12BProblem 24几何坐标几何坐标几何

Problem Statement

All three vertices of an equilateral triangle are on the parabola \(y=x^2\), and one of its sides has a slope of \(2\). The \(x\)-coordinates of the three vertices have a sum of \(\frac{m}{n}\), where \(m\) and \(n\) are relatively prime positive integers. What is the value of \(m+n\)?

Choices

  1. A14
  2. B15
  3. C16
  4. D17
  5. E18

Answer

A. 14

Solution

Step 1: Use the side with slope 2

Let the endpoints of the side with slope \(2\) have \(x\)-coordinates \(1-t\) and \(1+t\). This works because the slope between two parabola points is the sum of their \(x\)-coordinates, so the sum must be \(2\).

Step 2: Write the side vector

The two points differ by \((2t,4t)\). The midpoint of the side is \((1,1+t^2)\).

Step 3: Find the third vertex

Rotating half the side by \(90^\circ\) and scaling by \(\sqrt3\), the third vertex has coordinates \[(1-2\sqrt3\,t,\ 1+t^2+\sqrt3\,t)\] or the reflected version.

Step 4: Put the third vertex on the parabola

Using \(y=x^2\), \[1+t^2+\sqrt3\,t=(1-2\sqrt3\,t)^2.\] This simplifies to \(t(11t-5\sqrt3)=0\). Since the triangle is nondegenerate, \(t=\frac{5\sqrt3}{11}\).

Step 5: Sum the x-coordinates

The three \(x\)-coordinates sum to \[(1-t)+(1+t)+(1-2\sqrt3\,t)=3-2\sqrt3\cdot\frac{5\sqrt3}{11}=\frac{3}{11}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

Thus \(m=3\), \(n=11\), and \(m+n=\boxed{14}\).

Key Idea

Parameterize the side of slope \(2\) and rotate by \(60^\circ\).
