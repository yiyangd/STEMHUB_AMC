Problem Statement

On Halloween $31$ children walked into the principal's office asking for candy. They can be classified into three types: some always lie, some always tell the truth, and some alternately lie and tell the truth. The alternaters arbitrarily choose their first response, either a lie or the truth, but each subsequent statement has the opposite truth value from its predecessor. The principal asked everyone the same three questions in this order. First: \"Are you a truth-teller?\" The principal gave candy to each of the $22$ children who answered yes. Second: \"Are you an alternater?\" The principal gave candy to each of the $15$ children who answered yes. Third: \"Are you a liar?\" The principal gave candy to each of the $9$ children who answered yes. How many pieces of candy in all did the principal give to the children who always tell the truth?

Choices

  1. A7
  2. B12
  3. C21
  4. D27
  5. E31

Answer

A. 7

Solution

Step 1: Separate the three types

Let $T$ be the number of truth-tellers, $L$ the number of liars, and let the alternaters be split by their first response pattern. An alternater who starts truthfully will answer truth, lie, truth; one who starts with a lie will answer lie, truth, lie.

Step 2: Find each type's yes pattern

A truth-teller answers yes, no, no to the three questions. A liar answers yes, yes, no, because every true identity question is answered falsely. An alternater starting truthfully answers no, no, no, while an alternater starting with a lie answers yes, yes, yes.

Step 3: Use the third question first

Only alternaters who start with a lie answer yes to \"Are you a liar?\" Therefore there are $9$ such alternaters.

Step 4: Use the second question

The second question has $15$ yes answers. These come from the liars and the $9$ lie-first alternaters, so \[L+9=15,\] giving $L=6$.

Step 5: Use the first question

The first question has $22$ yes answers. These come from truth-tellers, liars, and the $9$ lie-first alternaters, so \[T+6+9=22.\] Thus $T=7$.

Step 6: Conclude

Each truth-teller received candy only on the first question, so the total candy given to truth-tellers is $\boxed{7}$.

Key Idea

Classify the answer patterns for truth-tellers, liars, and alternaters.
