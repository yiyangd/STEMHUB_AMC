Problem Statement

At a math contest, $57$ students are wearing blue shirts, and another $75$ students are wearing yellow shirts. The $132$ students are assigned into $66$ pairs. In exactly $23$ of these pairs, both students are wearing blue shirts. In how many pairs are both students wearing yellow shirts?

Choices

  1. A23
  2. B32
  3. C37
  4. D41
  5. E64

Answer

B. 32

Solution

Step 1: Use the blue-blue pairs first

There are $23$ pairs with two blue shirts, so these pairs use \[2\cdot23=46\] blue-shirt students.

Step 2: Find the mixed pairs

There are $57$ blue-shirt students total, so \[57-46=11\] blue-shirt students remain. Each of these must be paired with a yellow-shirt student, creating $11$ mixed pairs.

Step 3: Count yellow students left for yellow-yellow pairs

The mixed pairs use $11$ yellow-shirt students. Thus \[75-11=64\] yellow-shirt students remain.

Step 4: Pair the remaining yellow students

These $64$ students form \[\frac{64}{2}=32\] yellow-yellow pairs.

Step 5: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{32}$.

Key Idea

Track how many blue students are used in blue-blue and mixed pairs.
