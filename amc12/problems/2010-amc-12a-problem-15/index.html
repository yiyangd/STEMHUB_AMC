2010 AMC 12A Problem 15

2010AMC 12AProblem 15概率与统计组合概率概率计数

Problem Statement

A coin is altered so that the probability that it lands on heads is less than $\frac12$, and when the coin is flipped four times, the probability of an equal number of heads and tails is $\frac16$. What is the probability that the coin lands on heads?

Choices

  1. A$\frac{\sqrt{15}-3}{6}$
  2. B$\frac{6-\sqrt{6\sqrt6+2}}{12}$
  3. C$\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2}$
  4. D$\frac{3-\sqrt3}{6}$
  5. E$\frac{\sqrt3-1}{2}$

Answer

D. \frac{3-\sqrt3}{6}

Solution

Step 1: Name the probability

Let $p$ be the probability that the coin lands on heads. We are told $p<\frac12$.

Step 2: Translate equal heads and tails

In four flips, having an equal number of heads and tails means exactly two heads and two tails. The probability is \[\binom42p^2(1-p)^2=6p^2(1-p)^2.\]

Step 3: Use the given probability

The problem states \[6p^2(1-p)^2=\frac16.\] Dividing by $6$ gives \[p^2(1-p)^2=\frac1{36}.\] Since $0<p<1$, we can take the positive square root: \[p(1-p)=\frac16.\]

Step 4: Solve the quadratic

This gives \[p-p^2=\frac16,\] or \[p^2-p+\frac16=0.\] The quadratic formula gives \[p=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac23}}2=\frac{1\pm\frac1{\sqrt3}}2=\frac{3\pm\sqrt3}{6}.\]

Step 5: Choose the probability less than one half

The value with the plus sign is greater than $\frac12$, so we choose \[p=\frac{3-\sqrt3}{6}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is \[\boxed{\frac{3-\sqrt3}{6}}.\]

Key Idea

Set up the binomial probability of exactly two heads in four flips.
