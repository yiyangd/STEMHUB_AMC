2014 AMC 12A Problem 21

2014AMC 12AProblem 21数论整数构造整数构造

Problem Statement

For every real number $x$, let $\lfloor x\rfloor$ denote the greatest integer not exceeding $x$, and let \[f(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor(2014^{x-\lfloor x\rfloor}-1).\] The set of all numbers $x$ such that $1\le x<2014$ and $f(x)\le1$ is a union of disjoint intervals. What is the sum of the lengths of those intervals?

Choices

  1. A1
  2. B$\dfrac{\log 2015}{\log 2014}$
  3. C$\dfrac{\log 2014}{\log 2013}$
  4. D$\dfrac{2014}{2013}$
  5. E$2014^{1/2014}$

Answer

A. 1

Solution

Step 1: Separate the integer and fractional parts

The floor function tells us to look at one interval at a time. Let

\[k=\lfloor x\rfloor.\]

Because $1\le x<2014$, the integer $k$ can be any value from $1$ through $2013$. On the interval $k\le x<k+1$, the floor value is fixed, so the expression becomes easier to handle.

Step 2: Rewrite the inequality on one fixed interval

For a fixed $k$, we have

\[f(x)=k(2014^{x-k}-1).\]

The condition $f(x)\le1$ is therefore

\[k(2014^{x-k}-1)\le1.\]

Since $k>0$, this is equivalent to

\[2014^{x-k}\le1+\frac1k=\frac{k+1}{k}.\]

Step 3: Turn the exponential inequality into an interval length

Taking logarithms base $2014$ gives

\[x-k\le \log_{2014}\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right).\]

So the allowed values of $x$ in the $k$th interval run from $k$ to

\[k+\log_{2014}\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right).\]

Thus the length contributed by this $k$ is exactly $\log_{2014}\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)$.

Step 4: Add the lengths and look for cancellation

The total length is

\[\sum_{k=1}^{2013}\log_{2014}\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right).\]

A natural reason to use logarithms here is that a sum of logs becomes the log of a product. Therefore

\[\sum_{k=1}^{2013}\log_{2014}\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)=\log_{2014}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{2013}\frac{k+1}{k}\right).\]

Step 5: Finish the telescoping product

The product cancels almost completely:

\[\prod_{k=1}^{2013}\frac{k+1}{k}=\frac21\cdot\frac32\cdot\frac43\cdots\frac{2014}{2013}=2014.\]

So the total length is

\[\log_{2014}(2014)=1.\]

The answer is $1$.

Key Idea

Write $x$ as an integer part plus a fractional part, then use a telescoping logarithm sum.
