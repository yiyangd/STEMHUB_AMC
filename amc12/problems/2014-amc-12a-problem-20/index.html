2014AMC 12AProblem 20代数运算与化简代数化简

Problem Statement

In $\triangle BAC$, $\angle BAC=40^\circ$, $AB=10$, and $AC=6$. Points $D$ and $E$ lie on $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AC}$ respectively. What is the minimum possible value of $BE+DE+CD$?

Choices

  1. A$6\sqrt3+3$
  2. B$\frac{27}{2}$
  3. C$8\sqrt3$
  4. D$14$
  5. E$3\sqrt3+9$

Answer

D. $14$

Solution

Step 1: Recognize the broken-path structure

The expression

\[BE+DE+CD\]

is the length of a path from $B$ to $C$ that touches side $AC$ at $E$ and side $AB$ at $D$. When a shortest path reflects off lines, a standard way to handle it is to unfold the reflections into one straight segment.

Step 2: Reflect one endpoint across each side

Reflect $B$ across line $AC$ to a point $B'$, and reflect $C$ across line $AB$ to a point $C'$. Then

\[BE=B'E,\qquad CD=C'D.\]

So minimizing $BE+DE+CD$ is the same as minimizing the broken path

\[B'E+ED+DC'.\]

Step 3: Turn the minimum into a straight line

The shortest route from $B'$ to $C'$ through points $E$ and $D$ on the two sides occurs when $B',E,D,C'$ are collinear. Therefore the minimum value is simply

\[B'C'.\]

This is the key idea: reflection changes the path length but removes the bends.

Step 4: Find the angle between the reflected rays

Reflection preserves distance from $A$, so

\[AB'=AB=10,\qquad AC'=AC=6.\]

The original angle at $A$ is $40^\circ$. After the two reflections, the angle between rays $AB'$ and $AC'$ is

\[40^\circ+40^\circ+40^\circ=120^\circ.\]

Step 5: Use the Law of Cosines

Now apply the Law of Cosines in triangle $AB'C'$:

\[(B'C')^2=10^2+6^2-2(10)(6)\cos120^\circ.\]

Since $\cos120^\circ=-\frac12$,

\[(B'C')^2=100+36+60=196.\]

Step 6: Finish

Thus

\[B'C'=14.\]

So the minimum possible value of $BE+DE+CD$ is $14$.

Key Idea

Unfold the broken path by reflecting across the two sides of the angle.
