2002 AMC 12A Problem 11

2002AMC 12AProblem 11概率与统计平均数与数据集平均数数据

Problem Statement

Mr. Earl E. Bird leaves his house for work at exactly $8{:}00$ A.M. every morning. When he averages $40$ miles per hour, he arrives three minutes late. When he averages $60$ miles per hour, he arrives three minutes early. At what average speed, in miles per hour, should he drive to arrive precisely on time?

Choices

  1. A45
  2. B48
  3. C50
  4. D55
  5. E58

Answer

B. 48

Solution

Step 1: Use travel time rather than speed directly

Let the distance to work be \(d\) miles, and let the on-time travel time be \(t\) hours.

Step 2: Write the two given times

At $40$ mph, he is $3$ minutes late, so \[\frac d{40}=t+\frac{3}{60}.\] At $60$ mph, he is $3$ minutes early, so \[\frac d{60}=t-\frac{3}{60}.\]

Step 3: Subtract the equations

The difference between the two travel times is $6$ minutes, or \(\frac{1}{10}\) hour. Thus \[\frac d{40}-\frac d{60}=\frac1{10}.\]

Step 4: Solve for the distance

Since \(\frac1{40}-\frac1{60}=\frac1{120}\), we get \[\frac d{120}=\frac1{10},\] so \(d=12\).

Step 5: Find the on-time speed

At $60$ mph, the trip takes \(\frac{12}{60}=\frac15\) hour and arrives $3$ minutes early, so the on-time trip takes \(\frac15+\frac1{20}=\frac14\) hour. The needed speed is \[12\div\frac14=48.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{48}$.

Key Idea

Use the six-minute difference between two travel times.
