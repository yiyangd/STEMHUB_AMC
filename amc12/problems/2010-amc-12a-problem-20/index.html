2010 AMC 12A Problem 20

2010AMC 12AProblem 20组合数学递推与序列计数递推序列

Problem Statement

Arithmetic sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ have integer terms with $a_1=b_1=1<a_2\le b_2$ and $a_nb_n=2010$ for some $n$. What is the largest possible value of $n$?

Choices

  1. A2
  2. B3
  3. C8
  4. D288
  5. E2009

Answer

C. 8

Solution

Step 1: Write the arithmetic sequences

Let the common differences be $r$ and $s$, where $1<a_2\le b_2$ implies $1\le r\le s$. Then \[a_n=1+(n-1)r,\qquad b_n=1+(n-1)s.\]

Step 2: Introduce m=n-1

Let $m=n-1$. The condition $a_nb_n=2010$ means that $2010$ must be written as a product of two factors, both congruent to $1$ modulo $m$.

Step 3: Use factor pairs of 2010

List the factor pairs with smaller factor first: \[(2,1005),(3,670),(5,402),(6,335),(10,201),(15,134),(30,67).\] For each pair $(A,B)$, the possible $m$ must divide both $A-1$ and $B-1$.

Step 4: Find the largest possible m

Compute \[\gcd(A-1,B-1)\] for these pairs. The largest value occurs for \[(15,134),\] where \[\gcd(14,133)=7.\] The other pairs give only smaller gcds.

Step 5: Build the sequences

With $m=7$, we have $n=8$. The factors are \[15=1+7\cdot2,\qquad 134=1+7\cdot19,\] so valid arithmetic sequences exist.

Step 6: Conclude

The largest possible value of $n$ is \[\boxed{8}.\]

Key Idea

Convert arithmetic-sequence terms into factor pairs congruent to 1 modulo n-1.
