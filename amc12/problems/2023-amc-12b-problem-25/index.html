2023 AMC 12B Problem 25

2023AMC 12BProblem 25几何面积与相似面积比例

Problem Statement

A regular pentagon with area $\sqrt5+1$ is printed on paper and cut out. The five vertices of the pentagon are folded into the center of the pentagon, creating a smaller pentagon. What is the area of the new pentagon?

Choices

  1. A$4-\sqrt5$
  2. B$\sqrt5-1$
  3. C$8-3\sqrt5$
  4. D$\frac{\sqrt5+1}{2}$
  5. E$\frac{2+\sqrt5}{3}$

Answer

B. $\sqrt5-1$

Solution

Step 1: Recognize what each fold line is

Let $O$ be the center of the original regular pentagon, and let $A$ be one vertex. Folding $A$ to $O$ means the crease is the perpendicular bisector of $\overline{AO}$.

So the crease is perpendicular to $\overline{AO}$ and passes through the midpoint of $\overline{AO}$.

Step 2: Describe the smaller pentagon

Doing this for all five vertices creates five congruent crease lines. By rotational symmetry, the region left in the middle is also a regular pentagon.

Thus the new pentagon is similar to the original pentagon. We only need the linear scale factor.

Step 3: Compare apothems

Let $R$ be the circumradius of the original pentagon. The apothem of the original pentagon is

\[R\cos36^\circ.\]

Each crease line is halfway from $O$ to a vertex, so the apothem of the new pentagon is

\[\frac R2.\]

Step 4: Find the scale factor

Therefore the linear scale factor from the original pentagon to the new pentagon is

\[\frac{R/2}{R\cos36^\circ}=\frac{1}{2\cos36^\circ}.\]

For a regular pentagon,

\[\cos36^\circ=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}.\]

So the scale factor is

\[\frac{1}{2\cdot\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}}=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt5}=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}.\]

Step 5: Convert the linear scale to an area scale

Areas scale by the square of the linear factor. Hence the new area is

\[(\sqrt5+1)\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\right)^2.\]

Now

\[\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\right)^2=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.\]

Step 6: Finish the computation

Thus the area is

\[(\sqrt5+1)\cdot\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.\]

Multiplying,

\[\frac{3\sqrt5+3-5-\sqrt5}{2}=\frac{2\sqrt5-2}{2}=\sqrt5-1.\]

Therefore the answer is $\sqrt5-1$.

Key Idea

Each fold crease is a perpendicular bisector from a vertex to the center, so the new pentagon is similar to the original.
