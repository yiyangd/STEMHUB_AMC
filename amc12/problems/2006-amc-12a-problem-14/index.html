Problem Statement

Two farmers agree that pigs are worth \(\$300\) and that goats are worth \(\$210\). When one farmer owes the other money, he pays the debt in pigs or goats, with change received in the form of goats or pigs as necessary. For example, a \(\$390\) debt could be paid with two pigs, with one goat received in change. What is the amount of the smallest positive debt that can be resolved in this way?

Choices

  1. A\(\$5\)
  2. B\(\$10\)
  3. C\(\$30\)
  4. D\(\$90\)
  5. E\(\$210\)

Answer

C. \$30

Solution

Step 1: Interpret paying with change

Paying with animals and receiving change means the debt can be represented as a difference of values of pigs and goats.

Step 2: Write the possible debt values

A resolvable debt has the form \[300p-210g\] for some integers \(p\) and \(g\), possibly after switching signs. So every resolvable debt is an integer combination of \(300\) and \(210\).

Step 3: Find the smallest positive combination

The smallest positive integer combination of two numbers is their greatest common divisor. Since \[\gcd(300,210)=30,\] every resolvable debt is a multiple of \(30\).

Step 4: Show 30 can actually occur

We also need to know \(30\) is attainable. For example, \(300-210=90\), and \(300-3\cdot90\) is not the right expression, but Euclidean combinations show \(300-210=90\) and \(210-2\cdot90=30\), so \(30\) is attainable using integer combinations.

Step 5: Conclude

The smallest positive debt is \(\boxed{\$30}\).

Key Idea

Recognize that all resolvable debts are integer combinations of 300 and 210.
