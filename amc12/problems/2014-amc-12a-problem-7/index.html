Problem Statement

The first three terms of a geometric progression are $\sqrt3$, $\sqrt[3]{3}$, and $\sqrt[6]{3}$. What is the fourth term?

Choices

  1. A1
  2. B$\sqrt[7]{3}$
  3. C$\sqrt[8]{3}$
  4. D$\sqrt[9]{3}$
  5. E$\sqrt[10]{3}$

Answer

A. 1

Solution

Step 1: Use exponents for radicals

Rewrite the terms as \[3^{1/2},\quad 3^{1/3},\quad 3^{1/6}.\]

Step 2: Find the common ratio

The ratio from the first term to the second is \[\frac{3^{1/3}}{3^{1/2}}=3^{-1/6}.\] The ratio from the second to the third is also \[3^{1/6-1/3}=3^{-1/6}.\]

Step 3: Multiply once more

The fourth term is \[3^{1/6}\cdot3^{-1/6}=3^0=1.\]

Step 4: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{1}$.

Key Idea

Use exponent notation for radicals in a geometric progression.
