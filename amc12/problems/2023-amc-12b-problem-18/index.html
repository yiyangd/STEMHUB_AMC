2023 AMC 12B Problem 18

2023AMC 12BProblem 18概率与统计平均数与数据集平均数数据

Problem Statement

Yolanda's first-semester quiz average was $3$ points higher than Zelda's. Yolanda's second-semester average was $18$ points higher than her first-semester average and again $3$ points higher than Zelda's second-semester average. Which statement cannot possibly be true?

Choices

  1. AYolanda's quiz average for the academic year was 22 points higher than Zelda's.
  2. BZelda's quiz average for the academic year was higher than Yolanda's.
  3. CYolanda's quiz average for the academic year was 3 points higher than Zelda's.
  4. DZelda's quiz average for the academic year equaled Yolanda's.
  5. EIf Zelda had scored 3 points higher on each quiz, she would have had the same yearly average as Yolanda.

Answer

A. Yolanda's quiz average was 22 points higher

Solution

Step 1: Normalize Zelda's semester averages

Let Zelda's first-semester average be $z$. Then Yolanda's first-semester average is $z+3$. Yolanda's second-semester average is $z+21$, so Zelda's second-semester average is $z+18$.

Step 2: Think about yearly averages

Each student's yearly average is a weighted average of that student's two semester averages, with weights depending on how many quizzes they took.

Step 3: Find the possible ranges

Zelda's yearly average must lie between $z$ and $z+18$. Yolanda's yearly average must lie between $z+3$ and $z+21$.

Step 4: Bound the difference

The largest possible gap would be approached when Yolanda's quizzes are mostly second-semester and Zelda's are mostly first-semester, giving a gap less than or equal to $21$.

Step 5: Conclude

A $22$-point yearly gap is impossible, while the other relationships can be arranged with suitable weights. The answer is $\boxed{\text{A}}$.

Key Idea

Compare possible weighted yearly averages between semester averages.
