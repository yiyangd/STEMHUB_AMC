2012 AMC 12A Problem 15

2012AMC 12AProblem 15概率与统计几何概率几何概率面积

Problem Statement

A $3\times3$ square is partitioned into $9$ unit squares. Each unit square is painted either white or black with each color being equally likely, chosen independently at random. The square is then rotated $90^\circ$ clockwise about its center, and every white square in a position formerly occupied by a black square is painted black. The colors of all other squares are left unchanged. What is the probability that the grid is now entirely black?

Choices

  1. A$\frac{49}{512}$
  2. B$\frac7{64}$
  3. C$\frac{121}{1024}$
  4. D$\frac{81}{512}$
  5. E$\frac9{32}$

Answer

A. \frac{49}{512}

Solution

Step 1: Use rotation cycles

A $90^\circ$ rotation of the $3\times3$ grid has two four-cycles, the four corners and the four edge-centers, plus the center square fixed.

Step 2: Understand the final black condition

After rotation, a white square becomes black only if it lands in a position that was formerly black. So for each rotation cycle, we need every white square to land on a formerly black square.

Step 3: Count one four-cycle

For a four-cycle, the allowed colorings around the cycle are exactly the binary strings with no adjacent pair of the form black followed by white in the rotation direction after the operation. A direct cycle check gives $7$ allowed colorings out of $2^4=16$.

Step 4: Handle the center

The center maps to itself. It must already be black, giving probability $\frac12$.

Step 5: Multiply independent cycles

The two four-cycles are independent, so the probability is \[\left(\frac7{16}\right)^2\cdot\frac12=\frac{49}{512}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is \[\boxed{\frac{49}{512}}.\]

Key Idea

Analyze the 90-degree rotation cycles of the 3 by 3 grid.
