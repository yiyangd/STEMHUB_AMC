2005 AMC 12A Problem 14

2005AMC 12AProblem 14概率与统计组合概率概率计数

Problem Statement

On a standard die one of the dots is removed at random with each dot equally likely to be chosen. The die is then rolled. What is the probability that the top face has an odd number of dots?

Choices

  1. A\(\frac5{11}\)
  2. B\(\frac{10}{21}\)
  3. C\(\frac12\)
  4. D\(\frac{11}{21}\)
  5. E\(\frac6{11}\)

Answer

D. \frac{11}{21}

Solution

Step 1: Count equally likely outcomes

There are \(21\) dots on a standard die and \(6\) possible top faces after the die is rolled, so there are \(21\cdot6=126\) equally likely dot-removal and roll outcomes.

Step 2: Consider removing a dot from an odd face

If a dot is removed from a face with \(1,3,\) or \(5\) dots, that face becomes even. Then only \(2\) faces show an odd number of dots. There are \(1+3+5=9\) such dots, contributing \(9\cdot2=18\) favorable outcomes.

Step 3: Consider removing a dot from an even face

If a dot is removed from a face with \(2,4,\) or \(6\) dots, that face becomes odd. Then \(4\) faces show an odd number of dots. There are \(2+4+6=12\) such dots, contributing \(12\cdot4=48\) favorable outcomes.

Step 4: Compute probability

The total favorable outcomes are \(18+48=66\). Thus the probability is \[\frac{66}{126}=\frac{11}{21}.\]

Step 5: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{\frac{11}{21}}$.

Key Idea

Average over the removed dot and the die roll.
