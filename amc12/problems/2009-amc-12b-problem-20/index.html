2009 AMC 12B Problem 20

2009AMC 12BProblem 20代数运算与化简代数化简

Problem Statement

A convex polyhedron $Q$ has vertices $V_1,V_2,\ldots,V_n$ and $100$ edges. For each $k$, a plane $P_k$ cuts $Q$ so that it cuts only the edges meeting at $V_k$. The planes do not intersect inside or on $Q$. The cuts produce $n$ pyramids and a new polyhedron $R$. How many edges does $R$ have?

Choices

  1. A200
  2. B250
  3. C300
  4. D350
  5. E400

Answer

C. 300

Solution

Step 1: Interpret the cutting operation

Each plane cuts off one original vertex of $Q$. After all cuts, the remaining polyhedron $R$ is the result of truncating every vertex. We can count its edges by looking at what happens to original edges and to the new cut faces.

Step 2: Count edges coming from original edges

Every original edge of $Q$ is shortened but still leaves one edge on $R$. Since $Q$ has $100$ edges, this contributes $100$ edges to $R$.

Step 3: Count edges around the new faces

When a vertex $V_k$ is cut off, the new face has as many edges as the degree of $V_k$, because each original edge meeting $V_k$ is cut once. So the total number of new-face edges is the sum of all vertex degrees.

Step 4: Use the handshaking principle

In any polyhedron graph, the sum of the vertex degrees is twice the number of edges. Therefore \[\sum \deg(V_k)=2\cdot100=200.\]

Step 5: Add the two types of edges

The edges of $R$ consist of the $100$ shortened original edges plus the $200$ edges bordering the new cut faces. Thus \[100+200=300.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The new polyhedron has \[\boxed{300}\] edges.

Key Idea

Use vertex truncation and count edges by original edges plus cut-face edges.
