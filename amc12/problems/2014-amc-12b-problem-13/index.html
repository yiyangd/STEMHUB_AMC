2014 AMC 12B Problem 13

2014AMC 12BProblem 13几何面积与相似面积比例

Problem Statement

Real numbers $a$ and $b$ are chosen with $1<a<b$ such that no triangle with positive area has side lengths $1,a,b$ or $\frac1b,\frac1a,1$. What is the smallest possible value of $b$?

Choices

  1. A$\frac{3+\sqrt3}{3}$
  2. B$\frac{\sqrt5}{2}$
  3. C$\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}$
  4. D$\frac{3+\sqrt6}{2}$
  5. E3

Answer

C. \frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}

Solution

Step 1: Translate the first non-triangle condition

Since $1<a<b$, the only way $1,a,b$ fails to make a positive-area triangle is \[b\ge a+1.\]

Step 2: Translate the reciprocal condition

For \[\frac1b,\frac1a,1,\] the largest side is $1$. This triple fails to make a positive-area triangle when \[1\ge \frac1a+\frac1b.\]

Step 3: Minimize b

At the smallest possible $b$, both boundary conditions should be tight: \[b=a+1,\qquad \frac1a+\frac1b=1.\]

Step 4: Solve

Substitute $b=a+1$: \[\frac1a+\frac1{a+1}=1.\] This gives \[a^2-a-1=0,\] so \[a=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.\]

Step 5: Find b

Then \[b=a+1=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is \[\boxed{\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}}.\]

Key Idea

Use degenerate triangle inequalities for both side triples.
