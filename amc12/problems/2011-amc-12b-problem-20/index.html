2011 AMC 12B Problem 20

2011AMC 12BProblem 20几何圆与曲线圆曲线

Problem Statement

Triangle $ABC$ has $AB=13$, $BC=14$, and $AC=15$. The points $D$, $E$, and $F$ are the midpoints of $\overline{AB}$, $\overline{BC}$, and $\overline{AC}$ respectively. Let $X\ne E$ be the intersection of the circumcircles of $\triangle BDE$ and $\triangle CEF$. What is $XA+XB+XC$?

Choices

  1. A$24$
  2. B$14\sqrt3$
  3. C$\frac{195}{8}$
  4. D$\frac{129\sqrt7}{14}$
  5. E$\frac{69\sqrt2}{4}$

Answer

C. $\frac{195}{8}$

Solution

Step 1: Look for a point shared by both smaller circumcircles

When two circles are defined using midpoints of a triangle, a good first question is whether the circumcenter of the original triangle appears naturally. Let $O$ be the circumcenter of $\triangle ABC$.

Since $D$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$, the line $OD$ is the perpendicular bisector of $\overline{AB}$. Similarly, $OE$ is the perpendicular bisector of $\overline{BC}$.

Step 2: Show that O lies on the first small circle

Because $OD\perp AB$, we have $\angle BDO=90^\circ$. Because $OE\perp BC$, we have $\angle BEO=90^\circ$.

Thus both $D$ and $E$ lie on the circle with diameter $\overline{BO}$. Therefore $B,D,E,O$ are concyclic, so $O$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle BDE$.

Step 3: Show that O lies on the second small circle

The same reasoning works with $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{AC}$. We have $OE\perp BC$ and $OF\perp AC$, so

\[\angle CEO=\angle CFO=90^\circ.\]

Therefore $C,E,F,O$ are concyclic, and $O$ also lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle CEF$.

Step 4: Identify X and reduce the problem

The two given circles already share $E$. Their other intersection is $X$, and we have just found that $O$ is on both circles.

So $X=O$. This means

\[XA=XB=XC=R,\]

where $R$ is the circumradius of $\triangle ABC$.

Step 5: Compute the circumradius

The triangle has side lengths $13$, $14$, and $15$. Its semiperimeter is

\[s=\frac{13+14+15}{2}=21.\]

By Heron's formula, its area is

\[K=\sqrt{21\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6}=84.\]

The circumradius formula gives

\[R=\frac{abc}{4K}=\frac{13\cdot14\cdot15}{4\cdot84}=\frac{65}{8}.\]

Step 6: Finish the requested sum

Since all three distances from $X$ to the vertices are equal to $R$,

\[XA+XB+XC=3R=3\cdot\frac{65}{8}=\frac{195}{8}.\]

Therefore the answer is $\frac{195}{8}$.

Key Idea

Recognize the second intersection as the circumcenter of the original triangle, then compute the circumradius.
