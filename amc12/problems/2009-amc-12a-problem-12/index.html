2009 AMC 12A Problem 12

2009AMC 12AProblem 12数论进制与数字数字进制

Problem Statement

How many positive integers less than \(1000\) are \(6\) times the sum of their digits?

Choices

  1. A0
  2. B1
  3. C2
  4. D4
  5. E12

Answer

B. 1

Solution

Step 1: Check one-digit numbers

If a one-digit number \(u\) worked, then \(u=6u\), forcing \(u=0\). But the number must be positive, so there are no one-digit solutions.

Step 2: Check two-digit numbers

Let the number be \(10t+u\). The condition is \[10t+u=6(t+u),\] so \(4t=5u\). Since \(t\) and \(u\) are digits, the only possibility is \(t=5\), \(u=4\), giving \(54\).

Step 3: Rule out three-digit numbers

Let the number be \(100h+10t+u\). Then \[100h+10t+u=6(h+t+u),\] so \[94h+4t=5u.\] The left side is at least \(94\), while the right side is at most \(45\), impossible.

Step 4: Count

The only positive integer less than \(1000\) that works is \(54\).

Step 5: Conclude

The answer is \(\boxed{1}\).

Key Idea

Use digit equations for one-, two-, and three-digit numbers.
