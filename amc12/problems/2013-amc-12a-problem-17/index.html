2013 AMC 12A Problem 17

2013AMC 12AProblem 17概率与统计平均数与数据集平均数数据

Problem Statement

A group of $12$ pirates agree to divide a treasure chest of gold coins among themselves as follows. The $k^\text{th}$ pirate to take a share takes $\frac{k}{12}$ of the coins that remain in the chest. The number of coins initially in the chest is the smallest number for which this arrangement will allow each pirate to receive a positive whole number of coins. How many coins does the $12^\text{th}$ pirate receive?

Choices

  1. A720
  2. B1296
  3. C1728
  4. D1925
  5. E3850

Answer

D. 1925

Solution

Step 1: Track what remains

If the initial number of coins is $N$, then after the first pirate, the fraction remaining is $\frac{11}{12}N$. After the second pirate, it is multiplied by $\frac{10}{12}$, and so on.

Step 2: Find the twelfth pirate's share

The twelfth pirate takes all coins remaining after the first $11$ pirates, so the twelfth share is \[N\cdot\frac{11!}{12^{11}}.\]

Step 3: Choose the smallest N

To make all divisions work with whole numbers, the smallest useful $N$ must cancel the denominator of this fraction as much as possible. The common part of $11!$ and $12^{11}$ is \[\gcd(11!,12^{11}).\]

Step 4: Compute the uncanceled part

We have \[12^{11}=2^{22}3^{11}.\] Also $11!$ contains $2^8$ and $3^4$. After canceling these, the part of $11!$ left in the twelfth share is \[5^2\cdot7\cdot11=1925.\]

Step 5: Conclude

The $12^\text{th}$ pirate receives $\boxed{1925}$ coins.

Key Idea

Track the remaining fraction after each pirate and minimize the starting number by prime factors.
