2012 AMC 12A Problem 12

2012AMC 12AProblem 12几何坐标几何坐标几何

Problem Statement

A square region $ABCD$ is externally tangent to the circle with equation $x^2+y^2=1$ at the point $(0,1)$ on side $\overline{CD}$. Vertices $A$ and $B$ are on the circle with equation $x^2+y^2=4$. What is the side length of this square?

Choices

  1. A$\frac{\sqrt{10}+5}{10}$
  2. B$\frac{2\sqrt5}{5}$
  3. C$\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}$
  4. D$\frac{2\sqrt{19}-4}{5}$
  5. E$\frac{9-\sqrt{17}}{5}$

Answer

D. \frac{2\sqrt{19}-4}{5}

Solution

Step 1: Use symmetry

The square is tangent to the unit circle at $(0,1)$, so its side $\overline{CD}$ is horizontal at $y=1$. Let the square have side length $s$ and be centered on the $y$-axis.

Step 2: Write coordinates for A and B

The top vertices are then \[\left(-\frac{s}{2},1+s\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\frac{s}{2},1+s\right).\] These lie on the circle $x^2+y^2=4$.

Step 3: Use the circle equation

Substitute one top vertex: \[\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^2+(1+s)^2=4.\]

Step 4: Solve the quadratic

This gives \[\frac{s^2}{4}+1+2s+s^2=4,\] so \[5s^2+8s-12=0.\] The positive root is \[s=\frac{-8+\sqrt{64+240}}{10}=\frac{2\sqrt{19}-4}{5}.\]

Step 5: Conclude

The side length is \[\boxed{\frac{2\sqrt{19}-4}{5}}.\]

Key Idea

Place the square symmetrically in coordinates and use the larger circle equation.
