2010 AMC 12A Problem 24

2010AMC 12AProblem 24代数对数与指数对数指数

Problem Statement

Let \[f(x)=\log_{10}(\sin(\pi x)\cdot\sin(2\pi x)\cdot\sin(3\pi x)\cdots\sin(8\pi x)).\] The intersection of the domain of $f(x)$ with the interval $[0,1]$ is a union of $n$ disjoint open intervals. What is $n$?

Choices

  1. A2
  2. B12
  3. C18
  4. D22
  5. E36

Answer

B. 12

Solution

Step 1: Translate the domain condition

The logarithm is defined exactly when \[\sin(\pi x)\sin(2\pi x)\cdots\sin(8\pi x)>0.\] So we need to count intervals in $[0,1]$ where this product is positive.

Step 2: Use symmetry

Because \[\sin(n\pi(1-x))=(-1)^{n+1}\sin(n\pi x),\] the product has the same sign at $x$ and $1-x$. Therefore it is enough to count positive intervals in $(0,\frac12)$ and then double the result.

Step 3: List the zeros in the first half

In $(0,\frac12)$, the product is zero at fractions $\frac{k}{m}$ with $2\le m\le8$. The distinct zeros are \[\frac18,\frac17,\frac16,\frac15,\frac14,\frac27,\frac13,\frac38,\frac25,\frac37.\] These split $(0,\frac12)$ into $11$ smaller intervals.

Step 4: Know when the sign changes

At most zeros exactly one sine factor changes sign, so the whole product changes sign. The exceptions are \[\frac14=\frac28\quad\text{and}\quad \frac13=\frac26,\] where two factors vanish and the product does not change sign.

Step 5: Count positive intervals in the first half

Near $x=0$, all sine factors are positive. Moving across the zeros and changing sign except at $\frac14$ and $\frac13$ gives $6$ positive intervals in $(0,\frac12)$.

Step 6: Double by symmetry

The interval $(\frac12,1)$ contributes another $6$ positive intervals. Hence the total number is \[6+6=12.\] The answer is $\boxed{12}$.

Key Idea

Count sign intervals of a product of sine functions.
