Problem Statement

The graph of \[y=x^6-10x^5+29x^4-4x^3+ax^2\] lies above the line \[y=bx+c\] except at three values of $x$, where the graph and the line intersect. What is the largest of those values?

Choices

  1. A4
  2. B5
  3. C6
  4. D7
  5. E8

Answer

A. 4

Solution

Step 1: Convert the geometry of the graph into algebra

If the polynomial graph lies above the line except where it intersects, then the difference \[x^6-10x^5+29x^4-4x^3+ax^2-(bx+c)\] is always nonnegative and has three real zeros. Each zero must be a double root; otherwise the sign would change.

Step 2: Write the difference as a square

Let the three intersection $x$-values be $r,s,t$. Then the difference has the form \[(x-r)^2(x-s)^2(x-t)^2.\] This is \[(x^3-S_1x^2+S_2x-S_3)^2,\] where $S_1=r+s+t$, $S_2=rs+rt+st$, and $S_3=rst$.

Step 3: Compare the x^5 and x^4 coefficients

The coefficient of $x^5$ is $-2S_1=-10$, so $S_1=5$. The coefficient of $x^4$ is $S_1^2+2S_2=29$, so \[25+2S_2=29,\] giving $S_2=2$.

Step 4: Compare the x^3 coefficient

The coefficient of $x^3$ is \[-2S_3-2S_1S_2.\] Since this equals $-4$, we get \[-2S_3-20=-4,\] so $S_3=-8$.

Step 5: Find the three roots

The numbers $r,s,t$ are roots of \[z^3-5z^2+2z+8=0.\] Factoring gives \[(z-4)(z-2)(z+1)=0.\] Thus the three intersection values are $-1,2,4$.

Step 6: Conclude

The largest of these values is $\boxed{4}$.

Key Idea

Use the fact that touching a line from above creates double roots.
