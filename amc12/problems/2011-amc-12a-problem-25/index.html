2011 AMC 12A Problem 25

2011AMC 12AProblem 25几何面积与相似面积比例

Problem Statement

Triangle $ABC$ has $\angle BAC=60^\circ$, $\angle CBA\le90^\circ$, $BC=1$, and $AC\ge AB$. Let $H$, $I$, and $O$ be the orthocenter, incenter, and circumcenter of $\triangle ABC$, respectively. Assume that the area of pentagon $BCOIH$ is the maximum possible. What is $\angle CBA$?

Choices

  1. A$60^\circ$
  2. B$72^\circ$
  3. C$75^\circ$
  4. D$80^\circ$
  5. E$90^\circ$

Answer

D. $80^\circ$

Solution

Step 1: Start with the fixed angle information

Let

\[\angle ABC=B,\qquad \angle ACB=C.\]

Since $\angle A=60^\circ$, we have

\[B+C=120^\circ.\]

The condition $AC\ge AB$ means the side opposite $B$ is at least the side opposite $C$, so $B\ge C$. Thus $B\ge60^\circ$ and $C\le60^\circ$.

Step 2: Put O, I, and H on one circle

Three standard angle facts are useful here:

\[\angle BOC=2\angle BAC=120^\circ,\]

\[\angle BIC=90^\circ+\frac12\angle BAC=120^\circ,\]

and

\[\angle BHC=180^\circ-\angle BAC=120^\circ.\]

Thus the points $B,C,O,I,H$ all see chord $\overline{BC}$ under the same angle, so they are concyclic.

Step 3: Reduce the area problem

The triangle $BCO$ is fixed because $BC=1$ and $\angle BOC=120^\circ$. Therefore its area is fixed.

So maximizing the area of pentagon $BCOIH$ is the same as maximizing the area of quadrilateral $BOIH$ on this fixed circle.

Step 4: Find a built-in equality of chords

In $\triangle BOC$, we have $OB=OC$, so

\[\angle OCB=30^\circ.\]

Because $CI$ bisects $\angle C$, and because the altitude $CH$ makes a $30^\circ$ angle with $CA$, angle chasing gives

\[\angle OCI=30^\circ-\frac C2,\]

and

\[\angle ICH=30^\circ-\frac C2.\]

These equal inscribed angles subtend equal chords, so

\[OI=IH.\]

Step 5: Use the maximum-area idea on a fixed circle

On a fixed circle with endpoints $B$ and $O$ fixed, the quadrilateral $BOIH$ has the most area when the moving points divide the relevant arc as evenly as their constraints allow.

Since we already know $OI=IH$, the maximum occurs when the third chord is equal too:

\[OI=IH=HB.\]

This is the same principle that an inscribed polygon with fixed endpoints gains area when its intermediate vertices are spread evenly rather than clustered.

Step 6: Convert equal chords into the requested angle

Equal chords give equal arcs, so the corresponding angles at $C$ are equal:

\[\angle OCI=\angle ICH=\angle HCB.\]

But together these three angles make

\[\angle OCB=30^\circ.\]

Therefore each is $10^\circ$. In particular,

\[\angle ICH=30^\circ-\frac C2=10^\circ,\]

so $C=40^\circ$. Hence

\[B=120^\circ-C=80^\circ.\]

Therefore $\angle CBA=80^\circ$.

Key Idea

Show that B, C, O, I, and H are cyclic, then maximize an inscribed quadrilateral by spacing key chords evenly.
