2011 AMC 10A Problem 25

2011AMC 10AProblem 25几何面积与相似面积比例

Problem Statement

Let $R$ be a square region and $n\ge4$ an integer. A point $X$ in the interior of $R$ is called $n$-ray partitional if there are $n$ rays emanating from $X$ that divide $R$ into $n$ triangles of equal area. How many points are $100$-ray partitional but not $60$-ray partitional?

Choices

  1. A1500
  2. B1560
  3. C2320
  4. D2480
  5. E2500

Answer

C. 2320

Solution

Step 1: Turn the square into coordinates

Let the square have side length $1$, and let point $X$ have distances $x$, $1-x$, $y$, and $1-y$ from the four sides. We only need distances to the sides because each small triangle has base on a side of the square and height equal to the distance from $X$ to that side.

Step 2: Find the condition for one side

If the square is divided into $n$ equal-area triangles, each triangle has area $\frac1n$. Along a side at distance $h$ from $X$, a triangle with base length $b$ has area $\frac12bh$, so $b=\frac{2}{nh}$. Since the side length is $1$, the number of such equal triangles along that side is $\frac{1}{b}=\frac{nh}{2}$.

Step 3: Convert to integer conditions

Therefore $X$ is $n$-ray partitional exactly when $\frac{nx}{2}$, $\frac{n(1-x)}{2}$, $\frac{ny}{2}$, and $\frac{n(1-y)}{2}$ are integers. For even $n$, this is equivalent to requiring $\frac{nx}{2}$ and $\frac{ny}{2}$ to be integers.

Step 4: Count 100-ray points

For $n=100$, we need $50x$ and $50y$ to be integers. Since $X$ is inside the square, each coordinate can be $\frac1{50},\frac2{50},\ldots,\frac{49}{50}$, giving $49^2=2401$ points.

Step 5: Subtract the 60-ray points

For $n=60$, we need $30x$ and $30y$ to be integers. A point counted by both conditions must have both $50x$ and $30x$ integers, so $10x$ is an integer; similarly $10y$ is an integer. That gives $9^2=81$ points counted by both.

Step 6: Finish

The number that are $100$-ray partitional but not $60$-ray partitional is $2401-81=2320$. The answer is $\boxed{2320}$.

Key Idea

Translate equal-area ray partitions into integer conditions on the distances from the point to the square's sides.
