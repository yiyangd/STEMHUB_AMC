2011 AMC 10A Problem 10

2011AMC 10AProblem 10概率与统计平均数与数据集平均数数据

Problem Statement

A majority of the $30$ students in Ms. Deameanor's class bought pencils at the school bookstore. Each of these students bought the same number of pencils, and this number was greater than $1$. The cost of a pencil in cents was greater than the number of pencils each student bought, and the total cost of all the pencils was $\$17.71$. What was the cost of a pencil in cents?

Choices

  1. A7
  2. B11
  3. C17
  4. D23
  5. E77

Answer

B. 11

Solution

Step 1: Translate total cost into factors

The total cost was $\$17.71$, or $1771$ cents. This equals \[(\text{number of students})(\text{pencils per student})(\text{cents per pencil}).\]

Step 2: Use the majority condition

A majority of $30$ students means more than $15$ students bought pencils. So the number of buying students is an integer factor of $1771$ greater than $15$.

Step 3: Factor the total

The number factors as $1771=23\cdot77=23\cdot7\cdot11$. Since the pencil cost must be one of the answer choices, we can test which choice leaves a product that can include more than $15$ students and more than $1$ pencil per student.

Step 4: Find the consistent choice

If the cost is $11$ cents, then $1771\div11=161=23\cdot7$. This works: $23$ students each bought $7$ pencils, and $11>7$.

Step 5: Conclude

The cost of one pencil was $\boxed{11}$ cents.

Key Idea

Factor the total cost into students, pencils per student, and cents per pencil.
