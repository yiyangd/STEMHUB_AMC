2020 AMC 10A Problem 24

2020AMC 10AProblem 24数论进制与数字数字进制

Problem Statement

Let $n$ be the least positive integer greater than $1000$ for which \[\gcd(63,n+120)=21\] and \[\gcd(n+63,120)=60.\] What is the sum of the digits of $n$?

Choices

  1. A12
  2. B15
  3. C18
  4. D21
  5. E24

Answer

C. 18

Solution

Step 1: Translate the first gcd condition

Since $63=3^2\cdot7$, the condition \[\gcd(63,n+120)=21\] means $n+120$ is divisible by $21$ but not by $9$. In particular, \[n\equiv -120\equiv6\pmod{21}.\]

Step 2: Translate the second gcd condition

Since $120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5$, the condition \[\gcd(n+63,120)=60\] means $n+63$ is divisible by $60$ but not by $120$. Thus \[n\equiv57\pmod{60}\] and specifically \[n\equiv117\pmod{120}.\]

Step 3: Solve the congruences

Solve \[n\equiv6\pmod{21},\qquad n\equiv117\pmod{120}.\] Checking values $117,237,357,\ldots$ modulo $21$, the solutions are \[n\equiv237\pmod{840}.\]

Step 4: Find the least value over 1000

The least positive integer greater than $1000$ in this congruence class is \[237+2\cdot840=1917.\]

Step 5: Check the excluded divisibility

This value satisfies the required non-divisibility by $9$ and by $120$, so both gcd conditions are exact.

Step 6: Conclude

The sum of the digits is \[1+9+1+7=\boxed{18}.\]

Key Idea

Translate gcd conditions into congruences.
