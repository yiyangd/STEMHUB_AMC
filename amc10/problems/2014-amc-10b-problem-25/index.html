2014 AMC 10B Problem 25

2014AMC 10BProblem 25概率与统计独立性独立事件概率

Problem Statement

In a small pond there are eleven lily pads in a row labeled $0$ through $10$. A frog is sitting on pad $1$. When the frog is on pad $N$, where $0<N<10$, it jumps to pad $N-1$ with probability $\frac{N}{10}$ and to pad $N+1$ with probability $1-\frac{N}{10}$. If the frog reaches pad $0$ it is eaten by a snake; if it reaches pad $10$ it exits the pond. What is the probability that the frog escapes?

Choices

  1. A\frac{32}{79}
  2. B\frac{161}{384}
  3. C\frac{63}{146}
  4. D\frac7{16}
  5. E\frac12

Answer

C. \frac{63}{146}

Solution

Step 1: Set up escape probabilities

Let $p_i$ be the probability of eventually reaching pad $10$ before pad $0$ starting from pad $i$. We want $p_1$, with $p_0=0$ and $p_{10}=1$.

Step 2: Use the birth-death walk formula

For a one-dimensional walk with left probability $i/10$ and right probability $(10-i)/10$, \[p_1=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{9}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{j}{10-j}}.\]

Step 3: Simplify the products

The product is \[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{j}{10-j}=\frac{k!(9-k)!}{9!}=\frac1{\binom9k}.\]

Step 4: Evaluate the denominator

Thus \[\sum_{k=0}^{9}\frac1{\binom9k}=2+\frac29+\frac2{36}+\frac2{84}+\frac2{126}=\frac{146}{63}.\]

Step 5: Invert

Therefore $p_1=\frac{1}{146/63}=\frac{63}{146}$.

Step 6: Conclude

The frog escapes with probability $\boxed{\frac{63}{146}}$.

Key Idea

Use hitting probabilities for a one-dimensional birth-death random walk.
