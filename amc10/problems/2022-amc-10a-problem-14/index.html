2022 AMC 10A Problem 14

2022AMC 10AProblem 14组合数学排列组合排列组合计数

Problem Statement

How many ways are there to split the integers $1$ through $14$ into $7$ pairs such that in each pair, the greater number is at least $2$ times the smaller number?

Choices

  1. A108
  2. B120
  3. C126
  4. D132
  5. E144

Answer

E. 144

Solution

Step 1: Split small and large numbers

Numbers $8$ through $14$ cannot pair with each other, because then some small numbers would have to pair together and fail the condition. So each large number pairs with exactly one number from $1$ through $7$.

Step 2: Notice the forced pair

The number $7$ can pair only with $14$, because the partner must be at least $2\cdot7=14$. So $(7,14)$ is forced.

Step 3: Choose a partner for 6

Now $6$ can pair with $12$ or $13$, since $14$ is already used. That gives $2$ choices.

Step 4: Choose a partner for 5

After that, $5$ can pair with one of the remaining numbers among $10,11,12,13$. One of these may already have been used by $6$, leaving $3$ choices.

Step 5: Arrange the rest freely

The remaining small numbers $1,2,3,4$ can pair with the remaining four large numbers in any order, because each remaining large number is at least $8$.

Step 6: Multiply

The total number of pairings is \[2\cdot3\cdot4!=2\cdot3\cdot24=144.\] The answer is $\boxed{144}$.

Key Idea

Pair large numbers with small numbers in a forced order.
