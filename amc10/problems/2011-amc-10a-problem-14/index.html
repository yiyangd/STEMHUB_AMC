2011 AMC 10A Problem 14

2011AMC 10AProblem 14概率与统计几何概率几何概率面积

Problem Statement

A pair of standard $6$-sided fair dice is rolled once. The sum of the numbers rolled determines the diameter of a circle. What is the probability that the numerical value of the area of the circle is less than the numerical value of the circle's circumference?

Choices

  1. A\frac{1}{36}
  2. B\frac{1}{12}
  3. C\frac{1}{6}
  4. D\frac{1}{4}
  5. E\frac{5}{18}

Answer

B. \frac{1}{12}

Solution

Step 1: Translate diameter into radius

Let the dice sum be $s$. Then the circle has diameter $s$ and radius $\frac{s}{2}$.

Step 2: Compare area and circumference

The area is $\pi\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^2=\frac{\pi s^2}{4}$, and the circumference is $\pi s$. We need $\frac{\pi s^2}{4}<\pi s$.

Step 3: Simplify the inequality

Since $s$ is positive, divide by $\pi s$ to get $\frac{s}{4}<1$, so $s<4$. The only possible dice sums are therefore $2$ and $3$.

Step 4: Count dice outcomes

There is $1$ way to roll a sum of $2$ and $2$ ways to roll a sum of $3$, for $3$ favorable outcomes out of $36$ total.

Step 5: Compute the probability

The probability is $\frac{3}{36}=\frac{1}{12}$. The answer is $\boxed{\frac{1}{12}}$.

Key Idea

Convert the area-versus-circumference condition into a condition on the dice sum.
