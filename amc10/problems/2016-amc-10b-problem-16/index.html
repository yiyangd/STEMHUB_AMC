Problem Statement

The sum of an infinite geometric series is a positive number $S$, and the second term in the series is $1$. What is the smallest possible value of $S$?

Choices

  1. A$\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$
  2. B2
  3. C$\sqrt5$
  4. D3
  5. E4

Answer

E. 4

Solution

Step 1: Write the geometric series

Let the first term be $a$ and the common ratio be $r$. Since the second term is $1$, we have $ar=1$, so $a=\frac1r$.

Step 2: Use the sum formula

For an infinite geometric series, \[S=\frac{a}{1-r}=\frac{1}{r(1-r)}.\] The sum is positive and the series converges, so $0<r<1$.

Step 3: Minimize S by maximizing the denominator

To make $S$ as small as possible, maximize $r(1-r)$ for $0<r<1$.

Step 4: Maximize the quadratic

The expression $r(1-r)$ is largest at $r=\frac12$, where its value is $\frac14$.

Step 5: Compute the minimum

Thus the smallest possible sum is \[S=\frac{1}{1/4}=4.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{4}$.

Key Idea

Optimize the sum formula for a geometric series.
