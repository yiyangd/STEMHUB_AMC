Problem Statement

In how many ways can $6$ juniors and $6$ seniors form $3$ disjoint teams of $4$ people so that each team has $2$ juniors and $2$ seniors?

Choices

  1. A720
  2. B1350
  3. C2700
  4. D3280
  5. E8100

Answer

B. 1350

Solution

Step 1: Separate the juniors and seniors

Each team needs exactly $2$ juniors and $2$ seniors. It is natural to first divide the juniors into three pairs and divide the seniors into three pairs.

Step 2: Pair the juniors

The number of ways to partition $6$ juniors into $3$ unordered pairs is \[\frac{\binom62\binom42\binom22}{3!}=15.\]

Step 3: Pair the seniors

The same count applies to the $6$ seniors, giving another $15$ ways.

Step 4: Match junior pairs with senior pairs

After the junior pairs and senior pairs are formed, each team is made by matching one junior pair with one senior pair. There are \[3!=6\] matchings.

Step 5: Multiply the independent choices

The total number of team formations is \[15\cdot15\cdot6=1350.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{1350}$.

Key Idea

Pair juniors and seniors separately, then match the pairs into teams.
