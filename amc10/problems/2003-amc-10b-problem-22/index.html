Problem Statement

A clock chimes once at $30$ minutes past each hour and chimes on the hour according to the hour. For example, at $1$ PM there is one chime and at noon and midnight there are twelve chimes. Starting at $11{:}15$ AM on February $26$, $2003$, on what date will the $2003$rd chime occur?

Choices

  1. AMarch 8
  2. BMarch 9
  3. CMarch 10
  4. DMarch 20
  5. EMarch 21

Answer

B. March 9

Solution

Step 1: Look for a repeating block

The clock pattern repeats every $12$ hours. In a $12$-hour period, the hour chimes add to $1+2+\cdots+12=78$, and there are $12$ half-hour chimes.

Step 2: Count chimes per half-day and per day

Thus each $12$-hour block has $78+12=90$ chimes. Each full day has $180$ chimes.

Step 3: Remove full days

Starting at $11{:}15$ AM on February $26$, after $11$ full days the clock has chimed $11\cdot180=1980$ times. That brings us to $11{:}15$ AM on March $9$, with $2003-1980=23$ chimes left.

Step 4: Count the remaining chimes

From $11{:}15$ AM, the next chimes are: $11{:}30$ gives $1$, noon gives $12$ more for a total of $13$, $12{:}30$ gives $14$, $1{:}00$ gives $15$, $1{:}30$ gives $16$, $2{:}00$ gives $18$, $2{:}30$ gives $19$, $3{:}00$ gives $22$, and $3{:}30$ gives $23$.

Step 5: State the date

The $2003$rd chime occurs on March $9$, so the answer is $\boxed{\text{March 9}}$.

Key Idea

Count chimes in repeated 12-hour or 24-hour blocks, then handle the leftover chimes carefully.
