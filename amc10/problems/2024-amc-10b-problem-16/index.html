2024 AMC 10B Problem 16

2024AMC 10BProblem 16数论整数构造整数构造

Problem Statement

Jerry wrote all the integers from $1$ to $2024$ on a whiteboard. He repeatedly chose four numbers on the whiteboard, erased them, and replaced them with either their sum or their product. After repeatedly performing this operation, all the remaining numbers on the board were odd. What is the maximum possible number of integers on the board at that time?

Choices

  1. A1010
  2. B1011
  3. C1012
  4. D1013
  5. E1014

Answer

A. 1010

Solution

Step 1: Track the even numbers

Initially there are $1012$ even numbers and $1012$ odd numbers.

Step 2: See how an operation can produce an odd result

A product is odd only if all four chosen numbers are odd, which does not help remove even numbers. A sum is odd only if an odd number of the four chosen numbers are odd, so it can remove either $1$ or $3$ even numbers while producing an odd replacement.

Step 3: Get a lower bound on operations

One operation can eliminate at most $3$ even numbers. To eliminate all $1012$ even numbers, at least \[\left\lceil\frac{1012}{3}\right\rceil=338\] operations are needed.

Step 4: Convert operations to remaining count

Each operation replaces four numbers with one number, decreasing the board count by $3$. After at least $338$ operations, at most \[2024-3\cdot338=1010\] numbers can remain.

Step 5: Show the bound is possible

Use $337$ operations with $3$ evens and $1$ odd, removing $1011$ evens. Then use one operation with the last even and $3$ odds. Each replacement is an odd sum.

Step 6: Conclude

The maximum possible number remaining is $\boxed{1010}$.

Key Idea

Track parity and how many even numbers one operation can eliminate.
