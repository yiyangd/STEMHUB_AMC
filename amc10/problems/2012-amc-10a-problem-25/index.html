2012 AMC 10A Problem 25

2012AMC 10AProblem 25概率与统计独立性独立事件概率

Problem Statement

Real numbers $x,y,z$ are chosen independently and at random from $[0,n]$ for some positive integer $n$. The probability that no two of $x,y,z$ are within $1$ unit of each other is greater than $\frac12$. What is the smallest possible value of $n$?

Choices

  1. A7
  2. B8
  3. C9
  4. D10
  5. E11

Answer

D. 10

Solution

Step 1: Order the three numbers

There are $6$ possible orders for $x,y,z$, all equally likely except on boundary cases of probability zero. Work with one order, say $0\le z\le y\le x\le n$, and multiply is unnecessary because the ratio is the same in every order.

Step 2: Find the total ordered volume

The region $0\le z\le y\le x\le n$ is one sixth of the cube $[0,n]^3$, so its volume is $\frac{n^3}{6}$.

Step 3: Impose the spacing

In this order, no two numbers are within $1$ unit means $y-z>1$ and $x-y>1$. Shift by setting $z'=z$, $y'=y-1$, and $x'=x-2$. Then $0\le z'\le y'\le x'\le n-2$.

Step 4: Compute the favorable ratio

The favorable ordered volume is $\frac{(n-2)^3}{6}$. Therefore the probability is \[\frac{(n-2)^3}{n^3}.\]

Step 5: Test the threshold

For $n=9$, this is $\frac{7^3}{9^3}=\frac{343}{729}<\frac12$. For $n=10$, it is $\frac{8^3}{10^3}=\frac{512}{1000}>\frac12$. So the smallest possible $n$ is $\boxed{10}$.

Key Idea

Order the three random numbers and use a volume ratio.
