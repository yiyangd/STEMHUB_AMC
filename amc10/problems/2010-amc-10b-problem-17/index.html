2010 AMC 10B Problem 17

2010AMC 10BProblem 17概率与统计平均数与数据集平均数数据

Problem Statement

Every high school in the city of Euclid sent a team of $3$ students to a math contest. Each participant received a different score. Andrea's score was the median among all students, and hers was the highest score on her team. Andrea's teammates Beth and Carla placed $37^{\text{th}}$ and $64^{\text{th}}$, respectively. How many schools are in the city?

Choices

    Answer

    B. 23

    Solution

    Step 1: Translate schools into students

    Let there be $n$ schools. Since each school sends $3$ students, there are $3n$ contestants. Because all scores are different, the median is a single middle rank, so $3n$ must be odd and the median rank is $\frac{3n+1}{2}$.

    Step 2: Use Andrea's team condition

    Andrea has the highest score on her own team, while Beth and Carla placed $37$th and $64$th. Therefore Andrea's rank is better than both teammates, so her rank is less than $37$.

    Step 3: Use the existence of the 64th-place teammate

    There must be at least $64$ contestants, so $3n\ge64$. This gives $n\ge22$. Since $3n$ must be odd, $n$ must be odd, so possible nearby values begin with $23,25,\ldots$.

    Step 4: Test the median rank

    If $n=23$, then there are $69$ students and the median rank is $35$, which is less than $37$. That works with Andrea being ahead of Beth and Carla. If $n=25$, the median rank is $38$, which is not better than Beth's $37$th place, so it fails.

    Step 5: Conclude

    The only possible number of schools is $23$. Therefore the answer is $\boxed{23}$.

    Key Idea

    Use Andrea's median rank and teammate ranks to pin down the total number of students.
