2011 AMC 10A Problem 24

2011AMC 10AProblem 24几何空间几何空间几何体积

Problem Statement

Two distinct regular tetrahedra have all their vertices among the vertices of the same unit cube. What is the volume of the region formed by the intersection of the tetrahedra?

Choices

  1. A\frac{1}{12}
  2. B\frac{\sqrt2}{12}
  3. C\frac{\sqrt3}{12}
  4. D\frac{1}{6}
  5. E\frac{\sqrt2}{6}

Answer

D. \frac{1}{6}

Solution

Step 1: Visualize the two tetrahedra

A unit cube has two regular tetrahedra formed by choosing alternating vertices. These two tetrahedra are distinct and symmetric inside the cube.

Step 2: Identify the intersection

Their intersection is the octahedron whose vertices are the centers of the six faces of the cube. This is a common and useful way to see the central overlap of the two alternating tetrahedra.

Step 3: Split the octahedron

That octahedron can be split into two congruent square pyramids. The shared square is the middle cross-section of the cube, and its side length is $\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$, so its area is $\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)^2=\frac12$.

Step 4: Compute the volume

Each pyramid has height $\frac12$. So the total octahedron volume is \[2\cdot\frac13\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac16.\]

Step 5: Conclude

The intersection volume is $\boxed{\frac16}$.

Key Idea

The intersection of the two cube tetrahedra is the octahedron with vertices at the face centers.
