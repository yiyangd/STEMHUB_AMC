Problem Statement

Elmer's new car gives $50\%$ better fuel efficiency, measured in kilometers per liter, than his old car. However, his new car uses diesel fuel, which is $20\%$ more expensive per liter than the gasoline his old car used. By what percent will Elmer save money if he uses his new car instead of his old car for a long trip?

Choices

  1. A20%
  2. B$26\frac23\%$
  3. C$27\frac79\%$
  4. D$33\frac13\%$
  5. E$66\frac23\%$

Answer

A. 20%

Solution

Step 1: Use cost per kilometer

Let the old car travel $1$ kilometer per liter and let gasoline cost $\$1$ per liter. Then the old cost is $\$1$ per kilometer.

Step 2: Apply the new efficiency

The new car is $50\%$ more efficient, so it travels $1.5$ kilometers per liter.

Step 3: Apply the new fuel price

Diesel costs $20\%$ more, so it costs $\$1.20$ per liter.

Step 4: Compute new cost per kilometer

The new cost per kilometer is \[\frac{1.20}{1.5}=0.80.\]

Step 5: Find savings

Going from $\$1.00$ to $\$0.80$ is a savings of $20\%$.

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{20\%}$.

Key Idea

Compare cost per kilometer after scaling efficiency and fuel price.
