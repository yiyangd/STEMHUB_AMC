2020 AMC 10A Problem 25

2020AMC 10AProblem 25概率与统计组合概率概率计数

Problem Statement

Jason rolls three fair standard six-sided dice. Then he chooses a subset of the dice to reroll. After rerolling, he wins if and only if the sum is exactly $7$. Jason always plays to optimize his chances of winning. What is the probability that he chooses to reroll exactly two of the dice?

Choices

  1. A$\frac7{36}$
  2. B$\frac5{24}$
  3. C$\frac29$
  4. D$\frac{17}{72}$
  5. E$\frac14$

Answer

A. \frac7{36}

Solution

Step 1: Think in terms of the initial roll

Jason chooses the reroll subset after seeing the three dice. For each initial roll, compare the best winning probability from rerolling $0,1,2,$ or $3$ dice.

Step 2: Use a complete classification

For the $216$ equally likely initial rolls, the optimal choice is unique. A direct classification by kept sum shows: reroll $0$ dice for $15$ rolls, reroll $1$ die for $132$ rolls, reroll $2$ dice for $42$ rolls, and reroll all $3$ dice for $27$ rolls.

Step 3: Explain the two-dice class

Rerolling exactly two dice is best when keeping one die creates a target sum for two dice with higher probability than any one-die or three-dice option. For example, keeping a $1$ needs the two rerolled dice to sum to $6$, which has probability $\frac5{36}$.

Step 4: Count favorable initial rolls

The classification gives $42$ initial rolls for which the unique optimal strategy rerolls exactly two dice.

Step 5: Compute probability

Since all $216$ initial rolls are equally likely, the probability is \[\frac{42}{216}=\frac7{36}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{\frac7{36}}$.

Key Idea

Classify initial dice rolls by which reroll subset gives the best probability.
