Problem Statement

Three different pairs of shoes are placed in a row so that no left shoe is next to a right shoe from a different pair. In how many ways can these six shoes be lined up?

Choices

  1. A60
  2. B72
  3. C90
  4. D108
  5. E120

Answer

A. 60

Solution

Step 1: First choose the L/R pattern

Ignore the shoe colors for a moment and look only at the pattern of left shoes and right shoes. Whenever an $L$ is adjacent to an $R$, they must be from the same pair.

Step 2: Count the simplest patterns

The patterns \(LLLRRR\) and \(RRRLLL\) each allow $12$ labelings of the three pairs, giving $24$ arrangements.

Step 3: Count the remaining valid side patterns

A systematic check of the remaining left-right patterns shows six more valid patterns: \(LLRRRL, LRRLLR, LRRRLL, RLLLRR, RLLRRL,\) and \(RRLLLR\). In each, the adjacency constraints force enough matching that exactly $6$ pair-labelings are possible.

Step 4: Add the cases

These six patterns contribute \[6\cdot6=36\] arrangements.

Step 5: Combine

The total number of valid lineups is \[24+36=60.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{60}$.

Key Idea

Count valid left-right patterns and pair labels.
