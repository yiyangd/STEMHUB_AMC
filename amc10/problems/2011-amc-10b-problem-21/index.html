2011 AMC 10B Problem 21

2011AMC 10BProblem 21数论整数构造整数构造

Problem Statement

Brian writes down four integers $w>x>y>z$ whose sum is $44$. The pairwise positive differences of these numbers are $1,3,4,5,6,$ and $9$. What is the sum of the possible values for $w$?

Choices

  1. A16
  2. B31
  3. C48
  4. D62
  5. E93

Answer

B. 31

Solution

Step 1: Use the largest difference

Since $w$ is the largest number and $z$ is the smallest, the largest pairwise difference must be $w-z=9$.

Step 2: Think in adjacent gaps

Let the adjacent gaps be $w-x$, $x-y$, and $y-z$. These three gaps add to $9$, and all pairwise differences are made by adding consecutive gaps.

Step 3: Find the possible gap patterns

The six differences must be $1,3,4,5,6,9$. The adjacent gaps have to be three of these numbers and sum to $9$. The two working orders are $(5,1,3)$ and $(3,1,5)$, which produce exactly the required six differences.

Step 4: Convert each pattern to w

For gaps $(5,1,3)$, the numbers are $w,w-5,w-6,w-9$. Their sum is $4w-20=44$, so $w=16$. For gaps $(3,1,5)$, the numbers are $w,w-3,w-4,w-9$, giving $4w-16=44$, so $w=15$.

Step 5: Add possible values

The possible values of $w$ are $16$ and $15$, and their sum is $31$. The answer is $\boxed{31}$.

Key Idea

Represent the four numbers by adjacent gaps and test the possible gap patterns.
