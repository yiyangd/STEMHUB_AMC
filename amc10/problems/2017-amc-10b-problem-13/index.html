Problem Statement

There are $20$ students participating in an after-school program offering classes in yoga, bridge, and painting. Each student must take at least one of these three classes, but may take two or all three. There are $10$ students taking yoga, $13$ taking bridge, and $9$ taking painting. There are $9$ students taking at least two classes. How many students are taking all three classes?

Choices

  1. A1
  2. B2
  3. C3
  4. D4
  5. E5

Answer

C. 3

Solution

Step 1: Separate exactly two and all three

Let $y$ be the number of students taking exactly two classes, and let $z$ be the number taking all three classes. The problem says $y+z=9$.

Step 2: Count class enrollments

The total class enrollments are \[10+13+9=32.\] A student taking exactly one class contributes $1$, exactly two contributes $2$, and all three contributes $3$.

Step 3: Compare to the number of students

Since there are $20$ students, the extra enrollments beyond one per student are \[32-20=12.\] Exactly-two students add $1$ extra each, and all-three students add $2$ extras each, so \[y+2z=12.\]

Step 4: Solve

Subtract $y+z=9$ from $y+2z=12$ to get $z=3$.

Step 5: Conclude

There are $\boxed{3}$ students taking all three classes.

Key Idea

Use inclusion-exclusion counts by exactly one, exactly two, and three classes.
