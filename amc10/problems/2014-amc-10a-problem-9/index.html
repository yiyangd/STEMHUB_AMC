2014 AMC 10A Problem 9

2014AMC 10AProblem 9几何三角形三角形角度

Problem Statement

The two legs of a right triangle, which are altitudes, have lengths $2\sqrt3$ and $6$. How long is the third altitude of the triangle?

Choices

  1. A1
  2. B2
  3. C3
  4. D4
  5. E5

Answer

C. 3

Solution

Step 1: Use the two given legs

The right triangle has legs $2\sqrt3$ and $6$. Its area is \[\frac12(2\sqrt3)(6)=6\sqrt3.\]

Step 2: Find the hypotenuse

By the Pythagorean Theorem, the hypotenuse is \[\sqrt{(2\sqrt3)^2+6^2}=\sqrt{12+36}=4\sqrt3.\]

Step 3: Use area again

The third altitude is the altitude to the hypotenuse. If its length is $h$, then the area is also $\frac12(4\sqrt3)h$.

Step 4: Solve for h

Set the two area expressions equal: \[\frac12(4\sqrt3)h=6\sqrt3.\] Dividing by $2\sqrt3$ gives $h=3$.

Step 5: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{3}$.

Key Idea

Compute the area two ways to find the altitude to the hypotenuse.
