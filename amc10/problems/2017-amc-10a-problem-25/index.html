2017 AMC 10A Problem 25

2017AMC 10AProblem 25组合数学排列组合排列组合计数

Problem Statement

How many integers between $100$ and $999$, inclusive, have the property that some permutation of its digits is a multiple of $11$ between $100$ and $999$? For example, both $121$ and $211$ have this property.

Choices

  1. A226
  2. B243
  3. C270
  4. D469
  5. E486

Answer

A. 226

Solution

Step 1: Use the divisibility rule for 11

A three-digit number with digits $x,y,z$ is divisible by $11$ exactly when $x-y+z$ is $0$ or $11$. So for a multiset of three digits, we need one digit that can be placed in the middle so the other two digits minus it equal $0$ or $11$.

Step 2: Count the zero case

First count digit multisets where one digit equals the sum of the other two. These give $29$ valid multisets. Accounting for repeated digits and zeros, they produce $126$ three-digit integers.

Step 3: Count the eleven case

Next count digit multisets where the sum of two digits is $11$ more than the middle digit. These give $20$ valid multisets. Accounting for repeated digits and zeros, they produce $100$ three-digit integers.

Step 4: Explain the accounting

The adjustment is necessary because a multiset with three distinct nonzero digits gives $6$ three-digit integers, a multiset with a zero and two distinct nonzero digits gives $4$, and repeated digits give fewer distinct integers.

Step 5: Add the cases

The two cases are disjoint for three-digit digit multisets, so the total is \[126+100=226.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{226}$.

Key Idea

Use the divisibility rule for 11 and count digit multisets.
