2014 AMC 10B Problem 24

2014AMC 10BProblem 24组合数学排列组合排列组合计数

Problem Statement

The numbers $1,2,3,4,5$ are to be arranged in a circle. An arrangement is bad if it is not true that for every $n$ from $1$ to $15$ one can find a subset of the numbers that appear consecutively on the circle that sum to $n$. Arrangements that differ only by a rotation or a reflection are considered the same. How many different bad arrangements are there?

Choices

  1. A1
  2. B2
  3. C3
  4. D4
  5. E5

Answer

B. 2

Solution

Step 1: Use symmetry to limit the search

Up to rotation and reflection, there are only $12$ circular arrangements of $1,2,3,4,5$. A careful table of consecutive circular sums is small enough to manage.

Step 2: Know what must be checked

The total sum is $15$, so we need every sum from $1$ through $15$ to appear as a sum of consecutive entries around the circle.

Step 3: Find the bad arrangements

Checking the $12$ symmetry classes, exactly two fail: $(1,2,5,3,4)$ misses sums $6$ and $9$, and $(1,3,2,4,5)$ misses sums $7$ and $8$.

Step 4: Why the table is reliable

For each arrangement, there are only $5$ starting positions and at most $5$ lengths, so all consecutive circular sums can be listed directly without duplication worries.

Step 5: Conclude

There are $\boxed{2}$ bad arrangements.

Key Idea

Classify circular arrangements up to symmetry and check consecutive sums.
