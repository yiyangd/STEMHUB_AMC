2019 AMC 10B Problem 21

2019AMC 10BProblem 21概率与统计组合概率概率计数

Problem Statement

Debra flips a fair coin repeatedly, keeping track of how many heads and tails she has seen in total, until she gets either two heads in a row or two tails in a row, at which point she stops. What is the probability that she gets two heads in a row but she sees a second tail before she sees a second head?

Choices

  1. A$\frac1{36}$
  2. B$\frac1{24}$
  3. C$\frac1{18}$
  4. D$\frac1{12}$
  5. E$\frac16$

Answer

B. \frac1{24}

Solution

Step 1: Describe possible stopping strings

Before the stopping flip, the sequence cannot contain two equal consecutive flips. Therefore it must alternate. To stop with two heads in a row, the sequence must end in $HH$.

Step 2: Impose the second-tail condition

Debra must see her second tail before her second head. The shortest successful sequence is \[THTHH.\] The second tail occurs on flip $3$, while the second head occurs on flip $4$.

Step 3: List all successful patterns

After $THT$, Debra may continue alternating with pairs $HT$ any number of times, and then finish with $HH$. Thus the successful sequences are \[THTHH,\ THTHTHH,\ THTHTHTHH,\ldots\]

Step 4: Sum the probabilities

The probabilities are \[\frac1{2^5}+\frac1{2^7}+\frac1{2^9}+\cdots.\] This is a geometric series with first term $\frac1{32}$ and ratio $\frac14$.

Step 5: Compute

\[\frac{1/32}{1-1/4}=\frac{1}{32}\cdot\frac43=\frac1{24}.\]

Step 6: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{\frac1{24}}$.

Key Idea

List the alternating prefixes that allow HH to stop after the second tail comes first.
