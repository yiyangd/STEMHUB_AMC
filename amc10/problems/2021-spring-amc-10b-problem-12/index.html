Problem Statement

Let \[N=3^4\cdot3^4\cdot6^3\cdot27^0.\] What is the ratio of the sum of the odd divisors of $N$ to the sum of the even divisors of $N$?

Choices

  1. A$1:16$
  2. B$1:15$
  3. C$1:14$
  4. D$1:8$
  5. E$1:3$

Answer

C. 1:14

Solution

Step 1: Factor N

Since $27^0=1$ and $6^3=2^3\cdot3^3$, we have \[N=3^4\cdot3^4\cdot6^3\cdot27^0=2^3\cdot3^{11}.\]

Step 2: Separate odd and even divisors

The odd divisors are exactly the divisors of $3^{11}$, so their sum is \[\sigma(3^{11}).\]

Step 3: Find the even divisor sum

Even divisors must use a positive power of $2$. The sum of the possible powers of $2$ is \[2+4+8=14.\] For each such choice, the odd part can be any divisor of $3^{11}$.

Step 4: Compare the sums

Thus the sum of even divisors is \[14\sigma(3^{11}),\] while the odd divisor sum is \[\sigma(3^{11}).\]

Step 5: Conclude

The ratio is $\boxed{1:14}$.

Key Idea

Separate the odd divisor sum from the power of 2.
