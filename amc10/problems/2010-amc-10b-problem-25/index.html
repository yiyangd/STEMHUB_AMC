2010 AMC 10B Problem 25

2010AMC 10BProblem 25数论整数构造整数构造

Problem Statement

Let $a>0$, and let $P(x)$ be a polynomial with integer coefficients such that $P(1)=P(3)=P(5)=P(7)=a$, and $P(2)=P(4)=P(6)=P(8)=-a$. What is the smallest possible value of $a$?

Choices

    Answer

    B. 315

    Solution

    Step 1: Use roots at the odd inputs

    The condition $P(1)=P(3)=P(5)=P(7)=a$ means the polynomial $P(x)-a$ has roots $1,3,5,7$. Because these factors are monic with integer roots, we can write \[P(x)-a=(x-1)(x-3)(x-5)(x-7)Q(x)\] for some polynomial $Q(x)$ with integer coefficients.

    Step 2: Plug in the even inputs

    At $x=2,4,6,8$, we have $P(x)=-a$, so $P(x)-a=-2a$. The factor $(x-1)(x-3)(x-5)(x-7)$ takes the values $-15,9,-15,105$ at $x=2,4,6,8$.

    Step 3: Get a divisibility condition

    Because $Q$ has integer coefficients, $Q(2),Q(4),Q(6),Q(8)$ are integers. Therefore each of $15,9,15,105$ must divide $2a$. Their least common multiple is $315$, so $315$ divides $2a$. Since $315$ is odd, $315$ must divide $a$.

    Step 4: Show the lower bound is attainable

    It remains to know that $a=315$ is possible. One working choice is \[Q(x)=-8x^3+124x^2-576x+762.\] Then $Q(2)=42$, $Q(4)=-70$, $Q(6)=42$, and $Q(8)=-6$, exactly giving $P(x)-a=-630$ at the even inputs when $a=315$.

    Step 5: Conclude

    Thus $a$ must be a multiple of $315$, and $315$ can actually occur. The smallest possible value is $\boxed{315}$.

    Key Idea

    Use divisibility properties of integer-coefficient polynomials to force a lower bound, then construct it.
