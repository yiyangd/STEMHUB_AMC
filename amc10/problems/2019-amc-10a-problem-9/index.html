2019 AMC 10A Problem 9

2019AMC 10AProblem 9数论进制与数字数字进制

Problem Statement

What is the greatest three-digit positive integer $n$ for which the sum of the first $n$ positive integers is not a divisor of the product of the first $n$ positive integers?

Choices

  1. A995
  2. B996
  3. C997
  4. D998
  5. E999

Answer

B. 996

Solution

Step 1: Rewrite the divisibility question

The sum of the first $n$ positive integers is \[\frac{n(n+1)}2,\] and the product is $n!$. We need the greatest three-digit $n$ for which this sum does not divide $n!$.

Step 2: Check odd n

If $n$ is odd, then \[\frac{n(n+1)}2=n\cdot\frac{n+1}{2}.\] Both factors are at most $n$, and they are relatively prime, so this product divides $n!$.

Step 3: Check large candidates

The greatest three-digit number $999$ is odd, so it works as a divisor and is not the answer. The number $998$ is even, and \[\frac{998\cdot999}{2}=499\cdot999.\] The factors of $999$ are all present in $998!$, so this also divides $998!$.

Step 4: Find the first failure

For $n=996$, \[\frac{996\cdot997}{2}=498\cdot997.\] The factor $997$ is prime and greater than $996$, so it cannot appear in $996!$.

Step 5: Conclude

Therefore the greatest three-digit $n$ for which the sum does not divide the product is $\boxed{996}$.

Key Idea

Analyze when $ rac{n(n+1)}2$ divides $n!$.
