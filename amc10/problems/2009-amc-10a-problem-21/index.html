2009 AMC 10A Problem 21

2009AMC 10AProblem 21几何圆与曲线圆曲线

Problem Statement

Many Gothic cathedrals have windows with portions containing a ring of congruent circles that are circumscribed by a larger circle. In the figure shown, the number of smaller circles is four. What is the ratio of the sum of the areas of the four smaller circles to the area of the larger circle?

Choices

  1. A$3-2\sqrt2$
  2. B$2-\sqrt2$
  3. C$4(3-2\sqrt2)$
  4. D$\frac12(3-\sqrt2)$
  5. E$2\sqrt2-2$

Answer

C. 4(3-2\sqrt2)

Solution

Step 1: Name the radii

Let each small circle have radius $r$, and let the large circle have radius $R$.

Step 2: Use the four-circle geometry

The centers of the four small circles form a square. Adjacent small circles are tangent, so the side length of this square is $2r$. The distance from the center of the large circle to a small-circle center is half the diagonal, or $r\sqrt2$.

Step 3: Find the large radius

The large radius reaches from the center of the large circle to a small-circle center, then one more small radius to the outer tangency point. Thus $R=r\sqrt2+r=r(1+\sqrt2)$.

Step 4: Compare areas

The sum of the four small areas is $4\pi r^2$, and the large area is $\pi R^2=\pi r^2(1+\sqrt2)^2$. The ratio is $\frac{4}{(1+\sqrt2)^2}$.

Step 5: Simplify

Since $(1+\sqrt2)^2=3+2\sqrt2$, the ratio is $\frac{4}{3+2\sqrt2}=4(3-2\sqrt2)$.

Step 6: Answer

The ratio is $\boxed{4(3-2\sqrt2)}$.

Key Idea

Relate the small circle radius to the large circle radius using the square formed by the four centers.
