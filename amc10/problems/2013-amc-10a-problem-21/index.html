2013 AMC 10A Problem 21

2013AMC 10AProblem 21概率与统计平均数与数据集平均数数据

Problem Statement

A group of $12$ pirates divide a chest of gold coins as follows. The $k$th pirate to take a share takes $\frac{k}{12}$ of the coins that remain in the chest. The initial number of coins is the smallest number for which every pirate receives a positive whole number of coins. How many coins does the $12$th pirate receive?

Choices

  1. A720
  2. B1296
  3. C1728
  4. D1925
  5. E3850

Answer

D. 1925

Solution

Step 1: Track the remaining coins

Let $R_k$ be the number of coins left after the $k$th pirate takes a share. Pirate $k$ takes $\frac{k}{12}$ of $R_{k-1}$, so $R_k=\frac{12-k}{12}R_{k-1}$.

Step 2: Work backward

The $12$th pirate receives all of $R_{11}$. We want the smallest positive $R_{11}$ that makes every earlier remaining amount and every share an integer.

Step 3: Use the backward relation

For $k=11,10,\ldots,1$, the reverse formula is $R_{k-1}=\frac{12}{12-k}R_k$. Applying these divisibility requirements successively gives the smallest possible $R_{11}=1925$.

Step 4: Check the meaning

Since the $12$th pirate takes all coins remaining after the $11$th pirate, the $12$th pirate receives $R_{11}$.

Step 5: Conclude

The answer is $\boxed{1925}$.

Key Idea

Work backward through the divisibility conditions on the remaining coins.
