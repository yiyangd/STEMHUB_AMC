2006 AMC 10A Problem 25

2006AMC 10AProblem 25概率与统计独立性独立事件概率

Problem Statement

A bug starts at one vertex of a cube and moves along the edges of the cube according to the following rule. At each vertex the bug will choose to travel along one of the three edges emanating from that vertex. Each edge has equal probability of being chosen, and all choices are independent. What is the probability that after seven moves the bug will have visited every vertex exactly once?

Choices

  1. A$\frac{1}{2187}$
  2. B$\frac{1}{729}$
  3. C$\frac{2}{243}$
  4. D$\frac{1}{81}$
  5. E$\frac{5}{243}$

Answer

C. \frac{2}{243}

Solution

Step 1: Count all possible walks

At each of the $7$ moves, the bug has $3$ equally likely edge choices. So there are $3^7=2187$ possible move sequences.

Step 2: Understand the successful walks

To visit every vertex exactly once after $7$ moves, the bug must follow a Hamiltonian path of the cube starting from the initial vertex.

Step 3: Count first two moves by symmetry

From the starting vertex there are $3$ choices for the first move. From there, to avoid returning immediately, there are $2$ choices for the second move.

Step 4: Finish the path count

After two moves, the path lies on one square face of the cube. A careful continuation count gives $3$ possible completions from each such start: one continues around that face before crossing, and two cross earlier to the opposite face. Thus the number of successful paths is $3\cdot2\cdot3=18$.

Step 5: Answer

The probability is $\frac{18}{2187}=\boxed{\frac{2}{243}}$.

Key Idea

Count Hamiltonian paths on the cube from a fixed starting vertex.
